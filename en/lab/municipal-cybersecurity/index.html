ISSUE 03 · PUBLIC CYBERSECURITY & PRIVACY TELEMETRY All 2,809 Polish municipalities, counties & voivodeships Audit window: Nov 3–4, 2026 (34 days post-deadline) · ~13 min read

PLN 1.48B for “Cybersecure Local Gov”, yet 1 in 4 municipal email domains remain spoofable

Thirty-four days after the hard completion and settlement deadline of Poland's largest-ever municipal cybersecurity program — “Cyberbezpieczny Samorząd” (PLN 1.48 billion in EU FERC grants awarded across 2,511 local governments, hard deadline September 30, 2026) — and nearly three years after statutory email authentication became mandatory for every Polish public authority (December 25, 2023), 734 of 2,779 institutional municipal domains (26.41%) still fail to block forged emails sent in their name to citizens and accounting clerks. Exactly 362 local governments (13.03%) lack any valid DMARC record — including 62 municipalities awarded the maximum ~PLN 850k grant and major county-right cities Słupsk, Bytom, and Gorzów Wielkopolski — and the missing-DMARC rate among grant winners (12.77%) is statistically indistinguishable from non-recipients (15.20%, Yates χ² p = 0.28).

To test externally verifiable security and privacy hygiene across the entire country after grant settlement, I assembled a 100% census of Poland's 2,809 statutory local governments (2,479 municipalities, 314 counties, and 16 voivodeships), reconciled it to the exact penny against CPPC's grant ranking list (PLN 1,483,979,072.49), queried DNS mail records alongside 1,124 active .gov.pl mail domains, and ran clean stealth Chromium sessions across 470 municipal portals to record network requests fired at 0 seconds before any cookie-banner interaction.

Scope & fairness boundary · What external telemetry proves — and what it does not This study is a strictly passive, non-intrusive measurement of public internet surfaces (MX/SPF/DMARC/DNSSEC records per RFC 7489 & 7208, TLS handshakes, and zero-click browser requests on public homepages). It does not evaluate internal grant-funded upgrades — such as LAN segmentation, firewalls, offline backups, EDR endpoints, or staff training — which genuinely improve ransomware resilience behind the perimeter. What it proves is that a PLN 1.48B grant architecture failed to verify free, five-minute DNS protections required by Polish statute since December 2023.

Before diving into the numbers · Three hard baselines
  • 100% national census (2,809 of 2,809 units): after adding 2 statutory urban-rural municipalities omitted from MSWiA's contact spreadsheet (Włoszczowa and Baborów) and clearing 1 false county-level join, the dataset reconciles all 2,511 positive CPPC applications to the exact grosz (PLN 1,483,979,072.49 ranked; 2,495 signed contracts totaling PLN 1,474,312,573.61).
  • Mandatory by law since Dec 25, 2023: Article 24(1)–(2) of Poland's Electronic Communication Abuse Act of July 28, 2023 (Dz.U. 2023 item 1703) requires every public entity to use SPF, DMARC, and DKIM (3-month transition under Art. 40(1); administrative fines by the UKE President under Art. 27(7)).
  • Supreme Audit Office report P/25/003 (July 2026): NIK concluded that CPPC's grant KPIs measured purchase counts and training headcount rather than actual cybersecurity gains. Our DNS and browser census provides the nationwide empirical proof across all 2,809 units.
How missing DMARC differs from p=none and enforcing policies
  • Missing DMARC (or duplicate PermError): violates Article 24(2) outright — anyone on the internet can forge an email from taxes@your-town.pl without domain-level policy instructions to receiving mail servers.
  • Monitoring-only p=none: the office published a DNS text record, but explicitly instructs Gmail, WP, and Outlook: “even if authentication fails, deliver the forged message normally to the citizen's inbox.”
  • Enforcing p=quarantine or p=reject: real anti-spoofing protection — unauthenticated messages impersonating the municipal domain are quarantined as spam or rejected at the gateway.

Chapter 01 Billions for gear, zero for DNS

34 days after settlement, 62 municipalities with maximum ~PLN 850k grants still lack a free DMARC record

If a resident or local business in Bytom, Słupsk, or Gorzów Wielkopolski received an email this morning from podatki@um.bytom.pl, wydzial@um.slupsk.pl, or sekretariat@um.gorzow.pl announcing a new bank account for municipal tax or waste fees, none of those three city halls instructs receiving mail servers to block the forgery. All three county-right cities collected near-maximum or maximum grants under “Cyberbezpieczny Samorząd” — PLN 790,959, PLN 849,915, and PLN 850,000 respectively — and in early November 2026 none of them publishes a _dmarc DNS record.

Across an even wider tier of major regional authorities, a DMARC record exists on paper but sits in passive p=none mode, letting spoofed emails land directly in citizens' inboxes. That includes four of Poland's sixteen regional Marshal offices — Dolnośląskie (dolnyslask.pl, PLN 850k grant), Lubelskie (lubelskie.pl, PLN 779,972), Podkarpackie (podkarpackie.pl, PLN 850k), and Wielkopolskie (umww.pl, PLN 849,717) — alongside five more cities with county rights: Wrocław (um.wroc.pl, PLN 845,750), Zielona Góra (um.zielona-gora.pl, PLN 790,500), Jelenia Góra (jeleniagora.pl, PLN 850k), Piotrków Trybunalski (piotrkow.pl, PLN 804,714), and Przemyśl (um.przemysl.pl, PLN 850k). The sharpest test compares the 2,483 grant-winning authorities running institutional mail domains against the natural control group of 296 local governments that received no grant. Under Article 24(1)–(2) of the Electronic Communication Abuse Act of July 28, 2023 (Dz.U. 2023 item 1703), every Polish public authority has been legally required to deploy SPF, DMARC, and DKIM since December 25, 2023. Yet complete absence of effective DMARC stands at 12.77% among grant winners (317 of 2,483, holding PLN 171.85M in grants) versus 15.20% among non-recipients (45 of 296). That 2.44 percentage-point gap is statistically indistinguishable from zero (Yates χ² p = 0.2777; Fisher's exact p = 0.2357) — a null result that holds across every denominator specification.

Only when passive p=none policies are combined with missing records do grant winners show a modest advantage (25.45% spoofable vs. 34.46%, p = 0.0011), largely reflecting baseline staffing differences between larger applicants and tiny non-applicant communes. Even so, leaving 632 grant-winning offices (PLN 356.07M in public cybersecurity subsidies, including 150 max-grant units) open to trivial domain impersonation exposes a structural blind spot in invoice-driven grant compliance.

To make matters worse, three grant-winning municipalities (including Aleksandrów Kujawski — aleksandrowkujawski.pl, PLN 850k — and Sztum — sztum.pl, PLN 850k) publish SPF records exceeding RFC 7208's 10-lookup ceiling (PermError), while three institutional domains publish two conflicting v=DMARC1 TXT records simultaneously, forcing compliant receivers under RFC 7489 Section 6.6.3 to disable DMARC evaluation altogether.

The grant operator leaves 90% unquarantined The Digital Poland Project Center (cppc.gov.pl), which administered and settled the PLN 1.48B municipal cybersecurity program, publishes v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; sp=quarantine; pct=10 — leaving 90% of forged emails impersonating @cppc.gov.pl exempt from quarantine.
Chapter 02 Freemails, clouds & courts

30 town halls running official mail on @post.pl, @onet.pl, @gmail.com and shared @home.pl — and why Polish courts perform 10× better

Rather than trusting the Interior Ministry's contact spreadsheet blindly, I crawled the live official websites and Public Information Bulletins (BIP) of every municipality listed with a consumer or ISP mailbox. Three communes listed by MSWiA with legacy @wp.pl or @ns.onet.pl addresses had already migrated to their own domains with active DMARC quarantine — so our dataset credits them as protected institutional domains.

Even after that live verification, exactly 30 Polish municipal offices still publish a consumer freemail or shared ISP mailbox on their live websites in November 2026 as their primary official contact — 20 of them owning no institutional mail domain whatsoever, and 28 of 30 having collected “Cybersecure Local Gov” grants totaling PLN 12.61M. Two received the maximum PLN 850,000 grant: Ostrów Mazowiecka (gminaostrowmaz@home.pl) and Pisz (pisz@home.pl). The town of Kolno (PLN 814,377 grant) lists kolno@home.pl on its homepage while leaving its secondary mkolno.pl domain without DMARC. Others operate on Gmail (sekretariat.godkowo@gmail.com, PLN 226.7k), WP (ug.zawidz@wp.pl, PLN 490.2k), Tlen (ug.leoncin@tlen.pl, PLN 457.6k), five on @pro.onet.pl, eleven on @post.pl, and three on @data.pl. Mapping underlying MX mail hosts across all 2,779 institutional domains reveals an equally telling sovereignty split. Only 55 local governments (1.98%) route mail through Polish state or academic infrastructure (NASK / COI / PIONIER), whereas 92 authorities (3.31%) rely directly on US Big Tech cloud suites — 74 on Microsoft 365 / Azure (including Warsaw, Gdańsk, Katowice, Kielce, Gliwice, Toruń, Płock, and Koszalin) and 18 on Google Workspace — while shared commercial web hosts carry the bulk of local administration: home.pl / IONOS (597 units), nazwa.pl (297), and OVHcloud (128).

To check whether decentralized configuration failure is unique to municipalities, I ran the exact same DNS audit across all 1,395 registered .gov.pl domains, isolating 1,124 active mail-sending domains (switch to Tab 03 in Figure 01 above). The architectural lesson jumps off the chart: Courts and Prosecution offices (365 domains) achieve a 2.47% spoofable rate (only 9 of 365; 2.19% missing DMARC) because virtually every court routes mail through centralized Justice Ministry gateways (ms.gov.pl and prokuratura.gov.pl) secured once by a specialist team. Wherever central government leaves DNS configuration to individual offices, the exact same vulnerability reappears: 28.75% spoofable across ministries and central agencies (117 of 407), 36.76% across regional .gov.pl offices, and 3 of 4 active Sanitary Inspection mail domains.

DNSSEC & RFC 9116 security.txt Cryptographically validated DNSSEC (AD=True) protects just 376 of 2,779 municipal domains (13.53%), while a valid /.well-known/security.txt vulnerability disclosure file exists on exactly 1 of 2,809 local governments in Poland (Płock City Hall).
Chapter 03 Zero-Click citizen tracking

336 of 346 Meta-footprint municipal websites transmit citizen IPs and page URLs before any cookie-banner click

Public digital safety has two sides: preventing criminals from impersonating town hall, and ensuring town hall does not leak citizens' reading habits (from disability benefits and housing aid to tax arrears) to commercial ad-tech brokers. Static HTML inspection across reachable municipal websites detected Meta code on 350 portals. Static HTML alone, however, cannot prove a violation — a properly configured Consent Management Platform (CMP) might block those scripts until a visitor clicks “Accept.”

To test real runtime behavior, I launched clean headless Chromium sessions (with automation flags masked, fresh browser profiles, and zero clicks on any cookie banner) across all 350 Meta-footprint sites plus a 100-site control sample running Google Tag Manager without Meta code. Out of 346 reachable Meta-footprint sites, 336 (97.11% — including 293 Cybersecure grant winners awarded PLN 176.43M) opened live network connections to facebook.com or connect.facebook.net at 0 seconds, transmitting the citizen's IP address, full page URL, and browser fingerprint without consent.

Methodological rigor requires separating two distinct technical mechanisms within those 336 sites:

1. Unblocked Meta Social Plugins (331 local governments). Most offices (331 of 336) did not buy ad-retargeting campaigns; they embedded a live Facebook Page timeline widget (sdk.js / page.php) without consent gating. Legally, however, the Court of Justice of the European Union settled this exact architecture in its Grand Chamber judgment C-40/17 (Fashion ID, paras. 75–85 & 102): embedding a third-party social plugin that transmits visitor IP and browser strings on page load establishes joint controllership and requires prior legal basis before the script loads. For Polish public authorities, two rules close every loophole: Articles 399–400 of the Electronic Communications Law (Dz.U. 2024 item 1221) require prior opt-in consent for terminal storage/access, and GDPR Article 6(1) subparagraph 2 bars public authorities from invoking “legitimate interests” in the performance of their tasks.

2. Full commercial Meta Pixel ad-tracking (fbevents.js + facebook.com/tr) fired at 0 seconds (5 grant winners holding PLN 4.11M). Five local governments fire Meta's full commercial ad tracker and set _fbp identifiers pre-consent: Lubuskie Voivodeship (lubuskie.pl, PLN 850,000 grant, which simultaneously fires unconsented Hotjar session recording _hjSession), Gdynia (gdynia.pl, PLN 849,960.40), Tarnów (tarnow.pl, PLN 848,240.03), Polkowice (polkowice.eu, PLN 833,452.10), and Świdnik County (powiatswidnik.pl, PLN 724,298.14). On Tarnów's city portal, network capture caught Meta's Automatic Advanced Matching module (udwv[em]) loaded pre-consent — configured to SHA-256 hash email addresses typed into municipal forms for cross-matching against Facebook profiles. Meanwhile, across our 99 reachable GTM-only control portals, 54 (54.55%) fired Google Analytics 4 (g/collect) beacons at 0 seconds without consent.

Proof that lawful engineering is straightforward comes from 9 municipalities in the exact same Meta cohort — including Pruszcz Gdański, Trzebownisko, Mrągowo, Kożuchów, Iłowa, Nowe Ostrowy, Rudnik nad Sanem, Stawiguda, and Ustronie Morskie — whose consent managers held Meta and Google network requests at 0 until explicit opt-in. Conversely, the 86 municipalities hosted on the central state portal samorzad.gov.pl exhibit 0 Meta trackers and 100% HSTS on the web — yet 26 of those same 86 communes (30.23%) still run spoofable email on legacy .pl domains, and samorzad.gov.pl itself omits the mandatory mailto: URI scheme in its DMARC reporting tags (rua=dmarc@coi.gov.pl), causing RFC-compliant mail servers to drop aggregate attack reports.

30.7% broken HTTPS handshakes Out of 2,362 reachable municipal websites, 726 (30.74%) fail strict TLS certificate validation on default HTTPS connection (expired certificates, hostname mismatches on shared hosting, or missing intermediate chains).
Chapter 04 NIK diagnosis & 3 fixes

Why PLN 1.48B could be settled to the penny without deploying DMARC — and how to fix all three gaps in 30 days

How can a municipality collect PLN 850,000 for cybersecurity, pass a closing Information Security Management System audit, settle its EU grant before September 30, 2026, and still lack a DMARC record or a valid TLS certificate? Page 8 of Poland's Supreme Audit Office report from July 2026 (Implementation of the Cybersecure Local Government Project, audit P/25/003, ref. 98/2025/P/25/003/KAP) states the structural reason verbatim:

“The grant project output and outcome indicators defined by CPPC were not linked to an actual increase in the level of cybersecurity among grantees (local governments), but solely to the number of purchases made and persons trained.”

While NIK audited a targeted sample of ministries, project agencies, and municipalities — uncovering delayed calls and questionable eligible expenses such as diesel backup generators funded out of cybersecurity budgets — our census of all 2,809 Polish local governments supplies the nationwide empirical proof: procurement-driven compliance checklists are completely blind to automated, externally verifiable security posture.

Unlike multi-million-zloty server replacements, all three perimeter failures documented here can be fixed within 30 days at near-zero budget cost:

1. Automated DNS/TLS gating before final grant payout. Before disbursing final tranches of any public IT subsidy, an automated check should verify four non-negotiable baselines: valid SPF without PermError, enforcing DMARC (p=quarantine or p=reject at pct=100 with valid mailto: URIs), valid HTTPS + HSTS, and zero consumer freemail addresses in official registries.

2. Turnkey centralized mail relay modeled on Poland's Courts & Prosecution gateway. Just as 365 judicial domains achieved 97.5% anti-spoofing compliance via centralized Justice Ministry mail relays, COI/NASK should offer an opt-in turnkey mail gateway under samorzad.gov.pl (pre-configured with DKIM, DNSSEC, and p=reject) for the 30 freemail communes and hundreds of small rural offices lacking dedicated security engineers.

3. Stripping commercial ad pixels and click-to-load gating social widgets. Removing fbevents.js from municipal portals and placing Facebook widgets behind a two-click placeholder (as 9 compliant Polish municipalities already do) takes fifteen minutes — immediately ending pre-consent tracking of citizens seeking public services.

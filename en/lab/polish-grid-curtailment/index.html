ISSUE 04 · GRID TELEMETRY & DATA INVESTIGATION Polish Power System (PSE API v2 · 2024–2026) Telemetry window: Sep 24, 2024 – Nov 2, 2026 · ~14 min read

Shutting off cheap solar while power is expensive: what 73,924 quarter-hours of Poland's grid reveal

In popular shorthand, forced renewable curtailment is a sunny Sunday-afternoon phenomenon: demand drops, solar floods the grid, wholesale electricity prices turn negative, and dispatchers trim surplus zero-cost energy. On Wednesday, June 24, 2026 — an ordinary working weekday — Poland's transmission system operator curtailed 19.20 GWh of solar PV (peaking at 3,543.1 MW between 14:00 and 15:00, and still cutting 2,773.6 MW between 15:00 and 16:00 while the market settlement price stood at RCE = 312.1 PLN/MWh). Five hours later, at 20:00, wholesale prices spiked to 2,567.7 PLN/MWh (CSDAC = 2,698.7, CEN = 2,947.6 PLN/MWh), as the very coal and gas fleet that could not drop below 8.82 GW at noon had to climb to 17.30 GW.

To test how often Poland shuts down zero-marginal-cost renewables during expensive electricity hours — and what physically forces control rooms to do so — I joined 73,924 continuous 15-minute intervals across six primary API feeds of Polish TSO Polskie Sieci Elektroenergetyczne (2024-09-24 through 2026-11-02, zero missing quarters) with unit-level telemetry from 106 generating units across 37 power stations (gen-jw), physical and scheduled commercial flows across all six borders, and statutory compensation rules.

Glossary of PSE wholesale & balancing price benchmarks
  • RCE (Market Energy Price): Poland's 15-minute wholesale reference price (in 2026 identical across 100% of quarters to the Single Day-Ahead Coupling price CSDAC), used to credit dynamic net-billing solar prosumers.
  • CEN (Imbalance Settlement Price): the 15-minute Balancing Market settlement price (cen_cost in PSE's API). Under PSE's PV Financial Compensation Rules (March 2026, p. 2), CEN sets the statutory energy compensation price KC whenever system imbalance is negative.
  • Balance vs. network curtailment: balance redispatch reflects nationwide oversupply relative to export and pumping headroom; network redispatch reflects local line or transformer congestion.

Chapter 01 The price paradox

Beyond the negative-price Sunday myth: 58.6% of curtailed energy falls on working weekdays, and two-thirds at positive prices

Whenever forced solar and wind curtailment makes headlines in Central Europe, the standard explanation is reassuringly simple: on sunny spring Sundays, factory and office demand drops, rooftop and utility solar panels produce more electricity than the country needs, wholesale prices plunge below zero, and grid operators trim excess free energy. If that were the whole story, curtailment would be a benign weekend growing pain.

Joining PSE's 15-minute curtailment ledger (poze-redoze) interval by interval against wholesale settlement prices (rce-pln and energy-prices) tells a starkly different story. Across matched ten-month windows from January 1 through November 2, non-market renewable curtailment rose from 1,326.29 GWh in 2025 YTD to 1,805.05 GWh in 2026 YTD (+36.1% YoY) — comprising 1,262.60 GWh of solar PV (69.95%, up 29.5%) and 542.45 GWh of onshore wind (30.05%, up 54.4%). Remarkably, curtailment occurred across 27.3% fewer quarter-hours than a year earlier (6,993 active quarters vs. 9,623), meaning average curtailment depth during active intervals surged by 87.3% — from 551.3 MW to 1,032.5 MW.

The second surprise lies in PSE's own operational classification. Contrary to the widespread belief that renewables are throttled mainly because aging local distribution lines cannot carry rural solar output, 98.84% of all curtailed volume in 2026 YTD (1,784.03 GWh) was system-wide balance curtailment, while local network congestion accounted for just 1.16% (21.03 GWh — down from 2.24% in 2025 YTD; in full-year 2024, according to the Energy Regulatory Office's October 2025 statutory report, autonomous DSO-level curtailment across all 188 distribution operators totaled just 26.92 GWh alongside 722.34 GWh ordered by the TSO). The binding constraint is nationwide system inflexibility, not just local transformers.

The sharpest anomaly emerges once every curtailed quarter-hour is matched with its simultaneous wholesale price. In 2026 YTD, 58.56% of curtailed renewable electricity (1,056.97 GWh) was discarded Monday through Friday, while factories and offices were running (up 47.5% in volume from 716.72 GWh / 54.04% in 2025 YTD). Zero or negative wholesale prices (RCE ≤ 0 PLN/MWh) accounted for only 33.43% of curtailed volume (603.45 GWh across 1,361 quarters). Fully 66.57% — 1,201.60 GWh across 5,632 quarters — was shut off while wholesale electricity carried a positive market price.

Filtering strictly for expensive wholesale power — RCE ≥ 250 PLN/MWh (~58 EUR/MWh) — Polish grid operators curtailed 497.42 GWh of solar and wind in 2026 YTD (27.56% of total curtailment) across 3,276 quarter-hours (819 hours), nearly doubling 2025 YTD's 261.93 GWh (+89.9%). Above 400 PLN/MWh (~93 EUR/MWh), curtailed volume jumped 5.8-fold (+477.5%) from 31.76 GWh to 183.42 GWh (inspect all expensive weekday episodes in the explorer ↓). From June through October 2026, curtailment at RCE ≥ 250 PLN/MWh routinely represented 35% to over 50% of monthly discarded clean energy.

Fewer hours, nearly twice the megawatt depth With installed Polish solar PV surpassing 21 GW in 2026, dispatchers invoke curtailment in fewer intervals than in 2025, but slice 2.5–4.1 GW off the grid in a single command (peaking at 5,372.5 MW on Easter Sunday, April 5, 2026, and 4,065.5 MW on Wednesday, July 8, 2026).
Why prices stay high while solar is cut Day-ahead CSDAC/RCE clearing prices reflect the marginal bid of thermal coal and gas units required to clear the commercial schedule. When must-run thermal units set the marginal price, market prices remain elevated even as zero-cost renewables are curtailed non-marketly alongside them.
Chapter 02 Thermal floor

The 20:00 trap: why Bełchatów, Opole and Kozienice cannot shut down at solar noon

To understand why national grid dispatchers shut down three gigawatts of zero-marginal-cost solar power on a Tuesday or Wednesday afternoon while electricity costs 300 PLN/MWh, looking at noon is not enough. You have to look eight hours ahead — at 20:00.

In an official technical briefing to renewable producers on May 23, 2025 (slide 6), PSE spelled out the constraint explicitly: centrally dispatched thermal units (JG / JWCD) must maintain a minimum generation floor to satisfy "network and power-plant constraints, balancing reserves (FCR, FRR), short-circuit power, inertia (lessons from Spain / Portugal) and capability to cover the 6 GW evening peak." Invoking the Iberian blackout was grounded in synchronous grid physics: without rotating synchronous turbogenerators (or grid-forming inverters at scale), an AC grid loses rotational inertia and fault-clearing short-circuit current. Boiler thermodynamics is equally unforgiving: shutting down a pulverized-coal or lignite unit in the morning requires 8 to 16 hours to re-fire and synchronize — making it physically impossible to bring that capacity back for the evening peak after sunset.

Using PSE's unit-level gen-jw telemetry across 106 generating units at 37 power stations, I measured that floor plant by plant across nine representative 2026 weekdays of expensive curtailment (864 quarter-hours zero-filled across every station so offline plants never bias averages upward):

At 12:00 — right as solar PV averages 10.88 GW and dispatchers curtail 1.61 GW of renewables — pure thermal gen-jw output (lignite, hard coal, gas CCGT and CHP) bottoms out at 7,981.9 MW (7.98 GW), averaging 8,697.8 MW across midday RCE ≥ 250 PLN/MWh curtailment intervals (across all 3,276 expensive-curtailment quarters of 2026, system-wide JG averages 8,402 MW). Simultaneously, Poland's six pumped-storage hydro stations (Żarnowiec, Porąbka-Żar, Solina, Żydowo, Czorsztyn, Dychów) are already absorbing -775.8 MW in pumping mode until their upper reservoirs fill up.

Between 16:00 and 20:00, as solar PV collapses from 10.88 GW to 0.28 GW, the thermal fleet executes a massive upward climb. By 20:00, pure thermal gen-jw rises from 7,981.9 MW to 13,625.8 MW — a +5,643.9 MW (+5.64 GW) ramp: hard coal adds +2,522.5 MW (4.31 → 6.83 GW), lignite adds +1,996.8 MW (2.38 → 4.38 GW), gas CCGT adds +993.5 MW (1.03 → 2.02 GW), and CHP adds +131.2 MW. Adding the full net reversal of pumped-storage hydro — swinging from -775.8 MW midday pumping to +693.7 MW evening generation (+1,469.6 MW) — total controllable gen-jw net supply swings by +7,113.4 MW (+7.11 GW) in eight hours (on the system-wide his-wlk-cal JG series, which counts hydro generation but excludes pumping load, output rises from 7.54 to 13.83 GW, or +6.30 GW).

Inspecting individual stations (browse all 37 stations in the explorer below ↓) shows how each fleet carries that obligation: at Bełchatów (PGE's lignite flagship), an average of 8.8 of 12 units keep spinning through midday expensive cuts at throttled minimum load (1,812.9 MW at 12:00, 57.8% of evening output) so they can deliver 3,138.0 MW at 20:00 (+1,325.1 MW). Hard-coal Opole keeps 3.4 of 6 units online at 1,081.2 MW at noon to hit 2,001.6 MW at 20:00 (+920.4 MW); Kozienice 1 & 2 keep 5.5 of 11 units spinning (1,295.2 → 1,860.4 MW); Turów ramps 549.5 → 1,219.8 MW; and PGE's new CCGT gas units at Gryfino throttle down to 237.3 MW at noon (28.0% of evening load) before surging to 847.9 MW at 20:00 (+610.6 MW).

The tightest vise grip of the year occurred on Wednesday, June 24, 2026 (select Wed 2026-06-24 in Figure 02 below). Between 09:00 and 17:00, PSE curtailed 19.20 GWh of solar PV (7.99 GWh at RCE ≥ 250 PLN/MWh) while pure thermal gen-jw held an 8,822.1 MW floor at 12:00–13:00 (with -768.8 MW of ESP pumping). When the sun set into calm summer air (just 363 MW of wind nationwide at 20:00), pure thermal gen-jw had to leap to 17,295.4 MW in hour 20:00–21:00 (+8,473.3 MW hourly jump; or +9,360.0 MW from the 15-minute total gen-jw generation trough of 8,854.6 MW at 12:45 to 18,214.6 MW at 19:45). Even with virtually every available coal and gas boiler pushed near full output, scarcity drove 20:00–21:00 hourly prices to 2,567.7 PLN/MWh (RCE), 2,698.7 PLN/MWh (CSDAC), and 2,947.6 PLN/MWh (CEN; peaking at 3,016.0 PLN/MWh at 20:45–21:00).

Why legacy pumped-storage hydro saturates by midday Poland's six pumped-storage stations total ~1.8 GW of power and ~7–8 GWh of reservoir storage built decades ago for overnight coal baseload leveling. On a sunny 2026 day, a single morning of curtailed solar exceeds their entire upper-reservoir volume several times over.

Chapter 03 Six borders

What crosses Poland's borders during solar shutdowns: +605 MW of unscheduled German loop flows and commercial HVDC imports from the north

Public debate around cross-border electricity trade during renewable shutdowns tends to polarize into two half-truths: critics claim Poland is shutting down domestic solar farms because it is "flooded by foreign imports," while defenders reply that Poland is actually a net exporter during curtailment hours. Comparing 15-minute physical meter readings (przeplywy-mocy) against commercial day-ahead and intraday schedules (pwm-rdn-rdb, published by PSE since January 1, 2026) proves that aggregate national net figures hide two opposing border mechanisms.

Summed across all six borders simultaneously, during 2026 YTD expensive curtailment (RCE ≥ 250 PLN/MWh, 3,276 quarters), Poland is indeed an aggregate net exporter averaging -1,001.3 MW physically (and -1,094.2 MW commercially). Yet alternating current in Continental Europe's synchronous grid follows Kirchhoff's laws of impedance rather than commercial contracts. Disaggregating the six borders reveals why local grid stress looks very different from the national sum:

First, on the western synchronous AC border with Germany (DE) during those 3,276 expensive-curtailment quarters, commercial trade schedules average virtually zero (+2.1 MW), yet physical power flowing from Germany into western Poland averages +607.3 MW (with physical inflow into Poland in 71.4% of quarters, rising to +718.1 MW when RCE ≥ 400 PLN/MWh). That +605.2 MW gap is textbook unscheduled loop flow: wind and solar generation in northeastern Germany, constrained by internal German north–south bottlenecks, spills eastward through Polish 400/220 kV corridors and exits southward into Czechia (CZ: -1,183.8 MW physical vs. -445.4 MW commercial = -738.3 MW loop outflow) and Slovakia (SK: -502.8 MW physical). Even though transit enters on the west and exits on the south, it consumes physical thermal headroom on western Polish transmission corridors right as domestic wind and solar farms in Pomerania, Lubuskie and Greater Poland need export capacity.

Second, the exact opposite mechanism plays out on Poland's northern DC links — the subsea SwePol Link HVDC cable to Sweden (SE) and the LitPol Link HVDC interconnector to Lithuania (LT). Direct-current links carry virtually zero unscheduled loop flow (physical vs. commercial deltas stay within ±4 MW); every megawatt is dispatched by the European market-coupling algorithm flowing from lower-priced zones into higher-priced zones. Because Poland's must-run thermal floor keeps domestic clearing prices elevated above 250 PLN/MWh, the coupling algorithm reads Poland as a high-price sink — and deliberately schedules commercial imports into Poland from Lithuania (+79.3 MW commercial / +83.5 MW physical across 70.9% of quarters) and Sweden (+66.1 MW commercial / +62.7 MW physical), ramping even higher above 400 PLN/MWh (+107.6 MW from LT and +133.4 MW from SE) at the exact moment Polish dispatchers curtail domestic renewables non-marketly.

Moreover, across 862 curtailed quarter-hours in 2026 YTD (215.5 hours, 140.68 GWh of curtailed domestic RES, mean RCE = 441.9 PLN/MWh), Poland was an aggregate net physical importer across all borders combined (averaging +689.0 MW total net import, driven by +1,510.3 MW from Germany = +981.3 MW commercial import plus +529.0 MW loop inflow — filter net-import curtailment days below ↓).

Chapter 04 The bill & fixes

355M PLN on formula paper vs. 105.5M PLN in cash transfers, the :00 clock-hour guillotine, and household solar price scissors

Who pays for clean electricity ordered off the grid? Under Article 13(7) of EU Regulation 2019/943 and Article 9c(7a)–(7k) of Poland's Energy Law, eligible curtailed producers receive financial compensation from PSE, which recovers redispatch and balancing costs from every electricity consumer via the transmission quality fee (stawka jakościowa). Effective February 1, 2026, Poland's Energy Regulatory Office approved a quality fee of 33.16 PLN/MWh net (33.06 PLN/MWh in January 2026, up from 32.12 PLN/MWh in 2025 and more than triple 2021's 10.18 PLN/MWh).

Applying PSE's published PV Financial Compensation Rules (March 2026, p. 2) across the 1,805.05 GWh curtailed in 2026 YTD uncovers a striking three-way divergence. Valued at wholesale spot prices max(0, RCE), discarded 2026 YTD output is worth 251.46M PLN (or 254.27M PLN at max(0, CSDAC)). Under PSE's statutory formula for the market energy component KC, however, negative-imbalance intervals are priced at the Balancing Market settlement price max(0, CEN). Because Poland's balancing market was system-short (CEN ≥ 400 PLN/MWh) during 3,305 curtailed quarters in 2026, gross formula valuation reaches 355.09M PLN for ten months of 2026 alone (with just 29.52% of curtailed volume generating 296.12M PLN of that sum).

Compare that 355.09M PLN single-year gross figure against actual cash paid out: from the start of 2023 through July 2026, PSE transferred a cumulative 105.5M PLN to producers (19.5M in 2023, 2.7M in 2024, 25.4M in 2025, and 57.9M PLN in Jan–Jul 2026 — which PSE spokesperson Maciej Wapiński confirmed in August 2026 still covered 2023–2025 curtailments, alongside ~45M PLN of approved claims awaiting transfer). Four statutory and administrative filters explain that 3.4× gap:

First, an expanding share of newer solar and wind connections carry non-firm delivery clauses ineligible for compensation under Article 9c(7g) of the Energy Law and Article 13(7) of Regulation 2019/943 (as do exceedances on shared cable pooling connections under Article 7(9) PE). Second, under clause 2.1 of PSE's March 2026 rules (p. 2), energy sold through the statutory Obligated Seller receives KC = 0 PLN (recovering only lost auction or certificate support KAUK / KCERT on p. 3 and CfD volume accounting under Article 93(18) of the RES Act). Third, in 2,260 quarters (52.86% of volume), CEN ≤ 0 zeroes out KC. Fourth, multi-tier meter verification across 188 DSOs via PSE's WoR portal lags by many months: by summer 2026, Tauron had settled 36% of its 2025 claim value and Enea had approved 68% of 2025 applications.

A parallel gap between 15-minute market pricing and hourly physical dispatch shows up on the control-room clock. Poland adopted 15-minute balancing settlement on June 14, 2024. Yet inspecting all 2,339 large curtailment step-changes (|Δ| ≥ 200 MW between consecutive quarters across 2025–2026) reveals that 2,263 (96.75%) occurred precisely at minute :00 on the full clock hour, leaving just 23 at :15, 31 at :30, and 22 at :45 (76 off-hour adjustments across nearly two years). For steps ≥ 500 MW, the ratio is 1,136 out of 1,136 — 100.00% on the full hour. PSE's May 23, 2025 presentation (slides 18–20) explains why: balancing dispatch ran in hourly blocks with ~30 minutes of TSO lead time plus up to 1 hour for DSO execution. When cloud cover shifts or industrial load rises at :20, gigawatts of clean capacity remain pinned down until the next full hour even as 15-minute imbalance prices shoot past 400 PLN/MWh.

At the household end sit Poland's 1.61–1.65 million rooftop solar prosumers, where legal precision is essential: roughly one million pre-April 2022 households remain on 15-year volumetric net-metering, while April 2022 – June 2024 entrants stay on monthly average RCEm pricing unless they opt in under the November 27, 2024 RES Act amendment (Dz.U. 2024 item 1847). However, every new prosumer injecting power since July 1, 2024 is billed mandatorily on dynamic 15-minute RCE. Across 2026 YTD, 14.23% of all Polish PV output (3,101.05 of 21,791.31 GWh, peaking at 33.3% in April 2026) coincided with RCE ≤ 0 PLN/MWh, earning 0.00 PLN — because the statutory 1.23 deposit multiplier times zero is still zero. Across all 2026 YTD hours, PV-weighted max(0, RCE) averaged 324.21 PLN/MWh (398.77 PLN/MWh after the 1.23 deposit multiplier) versus 789.56 PLN/MWh wholesale RCE during the 18:00–22:00 evening peak (a 2.44× raw spot ratio, or 1.98× wholesale ratio after 1.23, before retail G11/G12 tariffs and distribution charges).

Three engineering levers that untie the 20:00 knot

The constructive takeaway from 73,924 quarter-hours is that Poland does not suffer from an annual renewable surplus — it suffers from an eight-hour flexibility bottleneck between 12:00 and 20:00. Three measurable upgrades address the root cause:

1. Completing 15-minute automated DSO dispatch (ending the :00 guillotine). Delivering sub-hourly SCADA/CSIRE curtailment release across distribution operators (targeted by PSE for 2026–2027) lets control rooms release curtailed farms at :15 or :30 as soon as system balance tightens, cutting expensive high-CEN curtailment immediately.

2. Utility-scale grid-forming batteries (BESS) replacing must-run thermal inertia. Every 1 GW of 4-hour grid-forming battery storage (providing synthetic inertia and fast FCR/aFRR reserves) works twice on the daily curve: absorbing 1 GW at noon instead of curtailing solar, and discharging 1 GW at 20:00 — trimming the required thermal ramp by 2 GW at once.

3. District-heating electrode boilers (Power-to-Heat) and western phase-shifting coordination. Absorbing midday surpluses into municipal thermal storage across Polish CHP networks while coordinating phase-shifting transformers and redispatch cost-sharing on the +605 MW German loop-flow corridor frees domestic grid capacity without hiking consumer transmission tariffs.

System mechanics, not conspiracy Telemetry shows rational actors responding to misaligned physical and market constraints: PSE holds thermal units online to safeguard inertia and the 20:00 peak, while European market coupling imports HVDC power from Lithuania and Sweden because those must-run thermal units keep Polish clearing prices above Nordic/Baltic levels.

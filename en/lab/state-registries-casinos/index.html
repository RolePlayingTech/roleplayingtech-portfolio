ISSUE 02 · INVESTIGATION & DATASET Public registries & illegal gambling (RSPO · RPWDL · MC · MF) Verified snapshot: Nov 3–4, 2026 · ~14 min read

How Polish state registries and .gov.pl pages funnel traffic to illegal offshore casinos

On the official website of Kłodzko County Office, the music schools directory lists the Secondary Music School in Bystrzyca Kłodzka. Clicking that link triggers an HTTP 301 redirect into learningpassport[.]pl — until spring 2025 the Polish teacher-training domain of an educational program run by UNICEF's Refugee Response Office in Poland, the Ministry of Education, the Educational Research Institute, the SOK Foundation and Microsoft, and for 527 days (as of November 3, 2026) an unblocked VOX Casino storefront accepting instant BLIK deposits.

To find out whether this was an isolated glitch or a structural pattern, I harvested and audited 35,583 unique registrable root domains across four Polish educational and healthcare registries (RSPO, RPWDL, Ministry of Family Nursery Registry) and cross-checked them against the Ministry of Digital Affairs statutory public websites list, all 59,376 entries of the Ministry of Finance Gambling Blocklist XML (2017–2026), DNS/RDAP telemetry, and Polish Wikipedia citations.

Chapter 01 Blind silos

Why offshore casinos abandoned disposable .xyz domains and started buying expired Polish schools, clinics and government campaigns

The trajectory of learningpassport[.]pl captures an industry-wide tactical pivot in miniature. Between June 2023 and April 2025, that auxiliary Polish domain served teacher-training courses for Learning Passport Polska — an education initiative run jointly by UNICEF's Refugee Response Office in Poland, the Ministry of Education, the Educational Research Institute (IBE), the SOK Foundation and Microsoft (whose core international course platform continues operating separately at poland.learningpassport.org ↗). When the Polish training cycle wound down in spring 2025 and its local domain registration lapsed, nothing placed the address in quarantine. Forty-five days after its final Wayback Machine capture, on May 25, 2025, the domain was re-registered through drop-catch marketplace Aftermarket.pl Limited, placed behind Cloudflare nameservers (dom + zainab), and turned into a Polish-language VOX Casino landing page advertising a 10,000 PLN welcome bonus. Soon after, the expired domain of a public music school in Bystrzyca Kłodzka (sm2bystrzyca[.]pl) was acquired as well, channeling visitors and search-engine PageRank straight from Kłodzko County's official directory at powiat.klodzko.pl/92/szkoly-muzyczne.html ↗. As of November 3, 2026 — 527 days later — neither domain appears on Poland's national gambling blocklist.

For the first seven years of Poland's gambling domain register (launched in April 2017), offshore operators played a disposable-mirror game: spinning up numbered domains under .com, .xyz, .top or .bet and incrementing the counter whenever customs officers blacklisted a subdomain. In 2023, across 4,391 newly blacklisted unique root domains (chronologically sorted and normalized via the full Public Suffix List), country-code .pl domains accounted for just 0.82% (36 roots) — or 0.70% (76 of 10,932) of raw XML rows. For search engines, however, fresh numbered domains carry zero authority and struggle to rank for lucrative Polish casino queries.

Starting in 2025, affiliate networks inverted their playbook. The .pl share of newly banned root domains jumped to 5.60% (124 of 2,216) in 2025 and reached 21.51% (931 of 4,328 roots — a 26.2× increase over 2023) between January 1 and November 3, 2026; in raw XML entries including subdomains, .pl rose 33.0× to 23.11% (1,731 of 7,491). Inspecting the 2025–2026 entries reveals more than 570 blacklisted .pl domains whose names have nothing to do with gambling: former primary schools, municipal welfare centers, cultural houses, public swimming pools, veterinary inspectorates and hospitals. To a search crawler, each of those domains comes pre-loaded with years of institutional dofollow backlinks from .gov.pl ministries, municipal Public Information Bulletins (BIP), regional school boards and Wikipedia.

If over five hundred hijacked Polish domains had already landed on the Ministry of Finance blocklist, the obvious question was the inverse: how many school, hospital and nursery domains are actively serving or promoting illegal casinos right now without being blocked — while official state registries still point citizens to them? Pulling primary dumps from four state registries and filtering out shared SaaS platforms yielded 35,583 unique registrable root domains:

Across active 2025/2026 RSPO schools and educational institutions, 105 of 14,286 dedicated non-MF domains (0.73%; 95% CI: 0.61%–0.89%; 0.76% including 4 MF-banned domains) actively served or promoted unlicensed online casinos. Disaggregating by ownership refutes the assumption that only small private schools lose control of their domains: public institutions stood at 0.78% (64 of 8,210) versus 0.67% among non-public ones (41 of 6,076; Fisher's exact p = 0.49). Across 8,198 historical domains from 2018–2023 RSPO archives, unblocked casino prevalence was more than twice as high at 1.70% (139 domains). Cross-checking all 139 against live rspo.gov.pl records showed that only 20.9% (29) belonged to liquidated schools; 66.9% (93) are still-operating schools that switched their website to edupage.org, szkolnastrona.pl or a municipal portal (only 3 moved to gov.pl) and let their old .pl expire. In Poland's Healthcare Providers Registry (RPWDL), 41 of 11,860 medical domains (0.35%) served unblocked casino content, and in the Ministry of Family Nursery Registry, 10 of 2,304 total dedicated nursery domains (0.43%: 6 of 1,386 non-RSPO roots plus 4 co-listed in RSPO).

Even more revealing is what happens across government databases after the Ministry of Finance officially bans a domain. Poland's blocklist operates as a one-way silo — no other state registry polls it (inspect all 68 MF-banned domains retained in public sources ↓):

First, on gov.pl/web/klimat/badz-odkrywca-parkow-narodowych ↗ and Biebrza National Park's bbpn.gov.pl/promocja-parkow-narodowych ↗, official pages still carry live dofollow links to odkrywcaparkownarodowych[.]pl — 657 days after the Ministry of Finance blacklisted it on January 16, 2025. Second, despite a 7-day statutory update duty under Art. 7(1)(10), Art. 7(2) and Art. 36(1)(1), (2)–(3) of the Educational Information System Act (Dz.U. 2026 item 803), the Ministry of Education's live rspo.gov.pl API still displays 9 MF-banned domains across 16 public institution cards — including two active public primary schools (RSPO 93026 ↗ in Różanki, zsrozanki[.]pl, banned Aug 21, 2026; and RSPO 48899 ↗ in Wola Rasztowska, spwolarasztowska[.]pl, banned Aug 13, 2026), one active non-public adult education center (dzierzno[.]pl, RSPO 48079 ↗ — 390 days post-ban) and 13 liquidated school cards. Third, in the e-Health Center's nightly RPWDL2 active-providers archive from November 3, 2026 (CSV_Rpm_aktywne), the MF-banned hospital domain pzzoz[.]bedzin[.]pl (banned March 4, 2026) was cleared from podmioty.csv yet continues shipping every night under active hospital book 000000014475 (Zagłębiowski Clinical Hospital) across 70 child rows: 2 historical facility records in zaklady.csv and 68 closed clinical ward records in komorki.csv (including ophthalmology and cytostatics). Fourth, the Ministry of Digital Affairs' statutory List of Public-Entity Websites under Art. 14(4) of the Digital Accessibility Act (dane.gov.pl #1875 ↗, latest published resource 65294 from March 13, 2025, data state April 30, 2024, 98,048 rows) currently distributes 159 verified casino domains: 22 already banned by MF and 137 unblocked.

Why Polish ISP DNS blocking doesn't stop SEO Polish telecoms redirect MF-banned domains to a Ministry warning screen for domestic users. Search crawlers (Googlebot, Bingbot), however, crawl from US and Irish IP ranges where Polish ISP sinkholing does not apply — so they index the full casino storefront powered by dofollow links from .gov.pl, BIP and Polish Wikipedia (39 MF-banned .pl domains across 72 articles).
9 official pages with live HTML links Beyond registry database fields, live HTML inspection confirmed 10 active dofollow <a href> tags across 9 official government and municipal pages (gov.pl/web/klimat, bbpn.gov.pl, powiat.klodzko.pl, and 6 municipal BIP pages — inspect all 9 pages ↓).
Chapter 02 Takeovers vs. victims

Why adding all 291 domains to the blocklist would cut patients off from county hospitals within 48 hours

Visit the website of the municipal health clinic in Cegłów east of Warsaw (spzozceglow[.]pl, linked directly from the town's official Public Information Bulletin at bip.ceglow.pl ↗) and you see an ordinary public health center: schedules for thirteen outpatient clinics, registration phone numbers, and an October 23, 2026 procurement notice for a Type A1 patient-transport ambulance. Only when you inspect the server's underlying subpages and HTML DOM do you find the parasite: eight days before the ambulance tender went up, an attacker exploited an unpatched CMS installation and injected a cluster of Polish-language doorway pages advertising no-deposit casino bonuses.

I found the exact same pattern on the live server of the County Hospital Complex in Żuromin (szpital-zuromin[.]pl — a public hospital listing 61 active wards, specialist clinics and diagnostic labs across 67 total rows in RPWDL), the Municipal Nursery in Starzyno (zlobekstarzyno[.]pl — 38 toddler places, 33 enrolled in Gmina Puck), an active four-school public complex in Warsaw (zslie1[.]edu[.]pl — 976 students and school announcements dated October 26, 2026), Public Preschool No. 337 in Warsaw (przedszkole337[.]pl, linked from przedszkole337.bip.warszawa.pl ↗ — where 67 hidden affiliate links sit in the HTML beside an October 30 parent workshop notice), Public Preschool No. 2 in Kłodzko (przedszkole2[.]klodzko[.]pl — 191 children, hosting an injected page targeting addicted gamblers searching how to cancel self-exclusion), Public Preschool in Mochy, and Arkady Fiedler Primary School in Gułtowy.

Forensic inspection of all 291 unblocked domains shows they split into three distinct technical vectors requiring opposite legal remedies:

Vector A (242 of 291 — 83.2%) consists of expired domains bought on the drop-catch market and repurposed into full offshore casino registration funnels with BLIK/crypto deposits or HTTP 301 redirects into 31 affiliate hub domains (for instance, sp109.bip.warszawa.pl/-/kontakt ↗ still points to sp109[.]waw[.]pl 552 days after its takeover, while Primary School No. 109 now operates on sp109warszawa.edupage.org). Fully 97.9% (237 of 242) sit behind Cloudflare.

Vector B2 (27 of 291 — 9.3%) comprises drop-caught domains turned into deceptive SEO facades (100% on Cloudflare). Rather than showing a roulette wheel on load, operators reconstructed 15 former school websites from Wayback Machine snapshots — preserving real principals' names, statutes and assembly photos while splicing casino links into AI-rewritten paragraphs (spnoskow[.]pl, spniemica[.]edu[.]pl, zsradzymin[.]pl, sp10szczecin[.]pl) — alongside 12 slot-review and crash-game facades such as Warsaw's przedszkole384[.]pl ("Aviator"), still linked on two przedszkole384.bip.warszawa.pl ↗ pages.

Vector B1 (22 of 291 — 7.6%) represents genuine live institutional servers compromised by third-party attackers (inspect all 22 Vector B1 victim servers ↓). These domains never expired: 95.5% (21 of 22) run on Polish or European shared hosting (cyber_Folks, home.pl, LH.pl, Hekko), where attackers hid gambling links via CSS (display: none, off-screen positioning, zero opacity) inside unpatched WordPress or Joomla sites. They include 9 public-sector servers (8 active public institutions today plus 1 legacy liquidated school server pspsycyna[.]pl) and 13 private clinics, schools and nurseries.

Crucial statutory boundary · Article 15f(4)–(5) & Article 29(4) of Poland's Gambling Act

All 269 drop-caught domains (Vector A + Vector B2) and their 31 redirect hubs exist solely to offer or advertise unlicensed gambling — making them textbook candidates for the Ministry of Finance DNS blocklist under Art. 15f(4)(1). Entering any domain into that register, however, triggers a mandatory 48-hour domain-wide ISP DNS block and tax-crime warning redirect under Article 15f(5) plus payment-provider cutoffs under Art. 15g(2), leaving an institution only a post-facto objection (sprzeciw) under Art. 15f(7) — while unwitting hack victims do not unlawfully promote gambling under Art. 29(4). Sinkholing szpital-zuromin[.]pl or spzozceglow[.]pl would sever patients from clinic schedules and tenders; those 22 servers belong in CERT Polska's (CSIRT NASK) incident-remediation queue.

Why per-host plugin versions are redacted During forensic probing of Vector B1 servers, I identified legacy PHP branches, exposed REST endpoints and vulnerable WordPress plugins. Publishing exact version numbers and injected URLs alongside named county hospitals would create a target blueprint — so per-host exploit details are stripped from the public JSON/CSV and reserved exclusively for CERT Polska.
The WordPress modified_gmt trap On przedszkole337[.]pl and klapciatek[.]pl, WordPress's date_gmt field showed 2019 and 2023 — the dates teachers originally created legitimate "About Us" and classroom pages. Comparing modified_gmt proved attackers injected the hidden casino links in October 2026.
Chapter 03 Bulk clusters

Cloudflare workspace fingerprints, four drop-catch registrars, and why 83% of hijacked domains survive past five months

Viewed one URL at a time, 269 hijacked educational and medical domains plus over five hundred previously banned .pl domains look like an anonymous fog behind Cloudflare's reverse proxy. Across the combined pool of 859 non-victim syndicate domains, 710 route through Cloudflare (82.7% overall, and 96.2% — 710 of 738 — among actively resolving domains), masking origin server IP addresses.

Cloudflare, however, leaves an organizational fingerprint in authoritative DNS: each account workspace is assigned a deterministic pair of nameservers drawn from roughly a thousand names (my DNS cache alone observed 574 distinct *.ns.cloudflare.com hostnames, implying a ~250,000-pair pool). Under independent account creation, the probability of thirty-four domains landing on the exact same nameserver pair by chance is below 10−120 (and still 7.5 × 10−55 even under an ultra-conservative 2,500-pair assumption).

Grouping DNS records exposes industrial-scale workspace clusters. The pair autumn.ns.cloudflare.com + wells.ns.cloudflare.com alone hosts 138 MF-banned Polish .pl domains. Meanwhile, grant.ns.cloudflare.com + uma.ns.cloudflare.com concentrates 34 domains on a single workspace: 31 still-unblocked Polish school, preschool and clinic domains from this audit, 1 unblocked redirect hub (puchalskidietetyk[.]pl), and 2 domains already blacklisted by the Ministry of Finance (budowajablonki[.]pl and personelgroup[.]pl) — with every live page serving identical VOX Casino templates (inspect all 31 unblocked grant+uma domains ↓). A similar bridge spans austin + sreeni (3 unblocked schools alongside 3 MF-banned ones, including spnasielsk[.]pl). Altogether, just five Cloudflare workspace accounts control 233 of 710 domains (32.8%). Two legal and methodological caveats apply: a shared NS pair proves a shared Cloudflare workspace (which may belong either to an in-house affiliate team or to a PBN agency provisioning domains for clients), and as a neutral network intermediary Cloudflare enjoys liability safe harbor under Articles 4–8 of the EU Digital Services Act (DSA) unless national authorities transmit formal action orders under Article 9 DSA.

A parallel bottleneck appears at domain acquisition. Querying NASK and IANA RDAP sequentially until NASK's HTTP 429 rate limit halted requests resolved 105 of the 291 unblocked domains (representing a 95.2% census of active non-.eu RSPO domains). Within that sample, four drop-catch registrars account for 78.1% of registrations (82 of 105; 79.6% after inverse-probability post-stratification): Cyprus-registered Aftermarket.pl Limited (39.0% overall, and 48.8% — 39 of 80 — within .pl Vector A takeovers), Dutch registrar Openprovider / Hosting Concepts B.V. (20.0%), France's Netim (11.4%), and US-based Dynadot (7.6%), alongside 23 domains spread across 13 other registrars. Importantly, automated EPP drop-catching itself is lawful: registrars execute backorders on expiring domains with strong backlink profiles because Polish registry rules currently release an expired public primary school domain into the open market after 30 days on the exact same footing as a defunct online shop.

Why the economics work so well becomes clear when we measure enforcement latency. Across 19 caught public-institution domains on the MF blocklist with RDAP registration timestamps, the median lag from drop-catch registration to MF ban was 131 days (IQR: 83–155 days; ranging from 28 days on piw[.]olawa[.]pl to 307 days on sudlugie[.]pl and 317 days on spnasielsk[.]pl). Meanwhile, across the 82 still-unblocked Vector A domains with RDAP dates, the median elapsed age is already 151 days (148 days among strict 2025–2026 drop-catch registrars, N = 77; max 816 days on zlobekgizycko[.]pl). Combining caught events and right-censored survivors in a Kaplan–Meier survival curve (N = 101) reveals the true enforcement window: 83.0% of hijacked public-institution domains remain unblocked at day 150, and 63.0% remain unblocked a full year (365 days) after takeover.

All 16 Polish voivodeships No region is spared: mapping Warsaw's przedszkolewola[.]pl to Mazowieckie places all 291 unblocked domains across all 16 provinces — led by Mazowieckie (59), Wielkopolskie (30), Małopolskie (26), Podkarpackie (24) and Śląskie (23), down to Opolskie and Świętokrzyskie (4 each) and Lubuskie (2).
Why Kaplan–Meier matters here Averaging lag solely across the 19 domains MF has already caught understates true casino survival because it ignores the 82 domains customs inspectors haven't caught yet (right-censored observations). Product-limit survival estimation incorporates both cohorts properly.
Chapter 04 Hygiene & fixes

Three traps caught during my internal code audit — and four low-cost architectural fixes that close the loophole

Just as in Issue 01's procurement audit, downloading official XML and CSV dumps was only half the work. Before freezing the 291 unblocked and 68 post-ban records, I ran a four-part verification pass across my own scripts and primary filings, catching three subtle traps inside the state datasets themselves.

First, the Ministry of Finance's mf_hazard.xml feed (59,376 entries) is ordered newest-first — from November 2026 at the top down to entry #1 from April 2017 at the bottom. Deduplicating root domains top-to-bottom without sorting ascending by DataWpisu credits multi-year casino domains to the last year a subdomain was banned, while omitting foreign cloud suffixes (vercel.app, co.uk, eu.com) inflates 2026's .pl share by 0.57 percentage points (22.08% vs. true 21.51%). Every time-series metric in this report uses the chronologically sorted Full PSL denominator.

Second, the e-Health Center's nightly RPWDL2 archive labeled CSV_Rpm_aktywne bundles closed historical clinic wards (with populated end-date columns) inside komorki.csv under active hospital books. That explains why pzzoz[.]bedzin[.]pl — removed from the main hospital record after its March 2026 MF ban — still ships nightly in 70 child facility and closed-ward rows under active hospital book 000000014475.

Third, filtering out domains shared by more than three RSPO units (designed to skip municipal school portals) initially pushed Warsaw's zslie1[.]edu[.]pl into the 2018–2023 historical bucket — even though those four units form one active public technical-school complex of 976 pupils. Live API verification restored it as the eighth active public institution in Vector B1.

Four low-cost engineering fixes instead of whack-a-mole domain chasing

Closing this structural gap does not require sweeping new legislation or expensive IT tenders — especially when keeping a legacy .pl domain parked on a 301 redirect costs ~100 PLN a year, whereas once drop-caught even a domestic DNS ban fails to stop US-based Googlebot from indexing its .gov.pl backlinks. Four lightweight safeguards across existing agencies would eliminate most of the attack surface:

1. Automated nightly cross-sync between MF XML, CERT Polska, and RSPO / RPWDL2 / Nursery Registry / MC #1875. MF's blocklist XML is already public and updated daily. A simple nightly job at CIE, CeZ and the Ministry of Digital Affairs comparing every Website field (including child tables zaklady.csv and komorki.csv) against MF and CERT lists — alongside enforcing rel="nofollow" or unlinking liquidated units — would purge banned drop-catches overnight and alert administrators.

2. NASK quarantine hold for expiring public-entity domains, .edu.pl eligibility checks, and clean decommissioning during gov.pl migrations. Fully 66.9% of hijacked historical RSPO domains belonged to still-active schools that moved to a new URL and let their old .pl lapse. Any domain listed in MC's statutory public-websites register (#1875) should carry a protective flag at NASK (with registrant eligibility verification for functional .edu.pl names): instead of dropping into public backorder auctions 30 days after an unpaid invoice, expired institutional domains should enter a non-registrable quarantine buffer with notice to the governing municipality and CERT Polska.

3. Cluster-level NS & template correlation at KAS — paired with strict Vector B1 victim routing to CERT Polska. When two VOX Casino domains on grant + uma hit the MF blocklist, automated NS-pair correlation (paired with formal Art. 9 DSA orders) can flag the remaining 31 hijacked school and clinic domains on that workspace in an afternoon rather than 151 days — while keeping live compromised institutional servers (Vector B1) off the MF register so Art. 15f(5)'s 48-hour DNS sinkhole never cuts off hospital patients, routing them instead to CERT Polska.

4. Weekly outbound link & RDAP-change scanner across .gov.pl and BIP portals. A lightweight crawler checking external links on ministry, national park, county and municipal BIP pages for HTTP 301 redirects, recent RDAP re-registrations, or MF/CERT matches would prevent ministry pages from linking to banned casinos for 657 days.

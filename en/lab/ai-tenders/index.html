ISSUE 01 · INVESTIGATION & DATASET Polish Public Procurement (BZP 2024–2026) Snapshot: 3 November 2026 · ~10 min read

What do Polish public institutions actually buy under the ‘AI’ label?

As Poland's national AI oversight rules came into effect in late October 2026, public debate split along familiar lines: official optimism about a digital leap on one side, and jokes that public-sector AI begins and ends with two-day ChatGPT workshops on the other.

Instead of arguing from headlines, I pulled 304 official notices from Poland's Public Procurement Bulletin API covering January 2024 to 3 November 2026, linked them by proceeding ID into 171 unique tenders, stripped out false positives and clerical errors, and audited every signed contract. All monetary figures below track signed gross contract values (often covering 12–36 months of licences, cloud support or phased deliveries) rather than single-year cash outlays.

Chapter 01 The headline illusion

Why public AI looks like endless prompt workshops on the surface, while the money goes to hospitals and labs

Scroll through raw tender titles in the Public Procurement Bulletin without opening the award protocols, and it is easy to conclude that Polish institutions mostly buy introductory AI workshops. Courses for regional civil servants, teacher trainings and two-day generative AI seminars appear almost every week. In the audited dataset, they account for 36 of the 159 core proceedings, or 22.6 per cent of all procurement attempts (inspect all 36 training tenders below ↓).

That impression flips as soon as you sum the signed gross contract values instead of counting notice titles. The median training contract in BZP is worth 47,000 PLN gross (around €11,000), and small individual lots closed below 2,000 PLN (1,482 PLN for a teaching workshop lot at the Polish Naval Academy in Gdynia; 1,750 PLN in Lubusz). All 26 signed training contracts across nearly three years add up to 2.46M PLN — just 7.6 per cent of core contract value. Another 9 research and advisory contracts add 0.82M PLN (2.6%, median 51.0k PLN).

Nearly nine out of every ten contracted zlotys — 89.8 per cent (28.92M PLN across 75 signed contracts) — go into two very different places: hospital IT departments and university research laboratories.

The largest bucket is AI software and operational deployments (17.17M PLN across 45 signed contracts, 53.3% of core value, median 255.3k PLN). Reading specifications chronologically from 2024 to November 2026 shows a shift from generic chat pilots toward narrow clinical and administrative systems. In February 2026 (award notices filed in early March), the Children's Memorial Health Institute in Warsaw signed two separate contracts: 365.3k PLN with Siemens Healthcare for AI-assisted MRI software, and 916.5k PLN with Orange Polska for interactive AI reception avatars (2026/BZP 00137744 ↗). The Regional Children's Rehabilitation Hospital in Ameryka near Olsztynek contracted a 1.28M PLN six-lot KPO package (2026/BZP 00124082 ↗) led by clinical documentation AI (490.8k PLN) and a virtual phone receptionist (165.0k PLN) alongside a dedicated AI server (471.3k PLN) and IT admin training. In Gliwice, the National Institute of Oncology paid 485.9k PLN to integrate its Agfa imaging archive with the national Intelligent Services Platform (2026/BZP 00195811 ↗). Meanwhile, Łowicz County signed a 680.7k PLN lump-sum contract with Agentic P.S.A. (2026/BZP 00379906 ↗, 5 bids) bundling a broader municipal Task Management System, Intranet portal, and staff trainings with a RAG chatbot designed to retrieve answers from internal county documents (filter all 62 software proceedings ↓).

The second pillar is GPU compute servers, diagnostic gear and lab robotics (11.74M PLN across 30 signed contracts, 36.5% of core value, median 171.4k PLN). Technical faculties, national institutes and regional branches of the National Health Fund (NFZ) are buying multi-GPU servers for local LLM inference (such as 622.4k PLN at the Wrocław NFZ branch), automated fluorescence microscopes (620.0k PLN at MIR-PIB in Gdynia), AI-assisted clinical ultrasound systems (332.0k PLN in Sochaczew, 199.0k PLN in Nowa Sól) and humanoid research robots in Częstochowa and Poznań (filter hardware & lab contracts ↓).

Because public buyers sometimes bundle AI into larger turnkey contracts, every core record carries an explicit scope tag. Pure-scope AI procurements account for 97 signed contracts worth 19.63M PLN (61.0% of core value, median 154.2k PLN; filter pure-AI contracts ↓). The remaining 13 mixed-scope contracts total 12.57M PLN (39.0%), where an inseparable lump sum or hybrid KPO package combines AI with construction works, heavy clinical equipment, physical services or general enterprise IT. The single largest core contract in the dataset — 4.94M PLN at Jan Długosz University in Częstochowa (2026/BZP 00163288 ↗) — is a design-and-build contract bundling architectural works, room construction and a CT scanner for its new Laboratory for AI in Diagnostic Imaging; the second-largest mixed contract, 2.16M PLN at Łukasiewicz–WIT in Warsaw (2025/BZP 00543355 ↗), combines 37 months of physical security guard services with AI-assisted access control.

Grouped by BZP notice publication year, signed core contract value rose 4.8×: from 4.05M PLN in 2024 (18 contracts across 24 proceedings) to 8.56M PLN in 2025 (35 across 41) and 19.59M PLN across the first ten months of 2026 (57 signed contracts across 94 proceedings to 3 November). Measured by physical contract-signing date instead — shifting three awards published in January 2024 (611.4k PLN) back to December 2023 — annual totals move from 0.61M PLN (Dec 2023, 3 contracts) and 3.44M PLN (2024, 15) to 8.95M PLN (2025, 37) and 19.19M PLN (2026, 55), a 5.6× jump between 2024 and 2026.

Who contracts the most Universities and research institutes lead with 15.24M PLN (47 signed contracts across 71 proceedings, 47.3%), followed by hospitals and healthcare providers at 11.36M PLN (31 contracts across 38 proceedings, 35.3%). Together they account for 26.60M PLN (82.6%) across 78 contracts — ahead of local governments and labour offices (2.70M PLN, 21 contracts), central agencies (2.70M PLN, 8) and schools (0.21M PLN, 3).
Silesia holds a strong #2 behind Masovia After fixing a 3× repeated Section 8.2 clerical total at Katowice's EMAG institute and isolating the AI lab lot at a school in Pawłowice, Masovia leads with 10.69M PLN (30 contracts, 33.2%) and Silesia stands a strong second at 8.66M PLN (14 contracts, 26.9% of the national total — well ahead of Lower Silesia's 2.00M PLN and Opole's 1.73M PLN), showing there is no Warsaw monopoly thanks to universities and hospitals in Częstochowa, Gliwice and Katowice (view Silesia in the explorer ↓).
Chapter 02 The bidding divide

Twenty-three bidders for a workshop lot, one bidder for a hospital deployment

The sharpest contrast in the dataset sits not in contract prices, but in the number of competing offers submitted per proceeding. It separates a crowded market for general trainings from a thin vendor pool in clinical and infrastructure deployments.

When Kielce University of Technology tendered a three-lot staff AI training worth 7,200 PLN in September 2026 (2026/BZP 00465518 ↗), its individual lots drew 23, 15 and 22 competing bids — a peak of 23 bidders on a single lot. (Because the same training firms routinely bid on parallel lots, the dataset records the maximum bid count on any single AI lot rather than summing bids across lots; non-AI lots in multi-topic tenders were likewise stripped out, such as removing burnout and Norma Pro courses from Lower Silesia's 2026/BZP 00304575 to leave AI Lot 2 at 16,684 PLN and 10 bids, or excluding a renewable-energy lab in Pawłowice's 2025/BZP 00133931 to leave the AI lab lot at 143.7k PLN and 6 bids.) An online AI course for Lower Silesian civil servants worth 29,750 PLN (2026/BZP 00428993) drew 22 bids, and a 1,750 PLN workshop in Lubusz attracted 14 bidders. Across all 24 competitive training contracts with disclosed bid counts, the median tender received six offers, and only 3 of 24 (12.5%) had a single bidder (sort trainings by bid count ↓).

Move into clinical software integrations, hospital pharmacy decision support, PACS imaging archives or on-premise security deployments, and the crowd disappears. Across signed AI software deployments with disclosed competitive bid counts, the median bid count drops to one: 27 of 41 contracts (65.9%) attracted only a single bidder (view all 27 single-bid software contracts ↓). Even the rare software exception underscores the pattern — when the Kraków Academy of Fine Arts tendered a standalone admissions chatbot for candidates (2026/BZP 00450838 ↗, 74.8k PLN), 19 vendors competed, whereas across healthcare as a whole 20 of 27 competitive contracts (74.1%) drew just one offer (and adding 4 sole-source awards, 24 of 31 signed hospital contracts worth 8.19M PLN faced zero competing bids).

Other buyers receive no bids at all or quotes above their grant ceiling. In March 2026, the 5th Military Clinical Hospital in Kraków cancelled tender 2026/BZP 00155059 for laboratory diagnostics AI software after zero bids arrived by the deadline. Overall, 31 of the 159 core proceedings (19.5%) — and 32 across all 171 proceedings — ended in cancellation, often on first attempts to buy GPU servers or clinical integrations.

Earlier this year I wrote an essay (in Polish, “Blue Oceans of Artificial Intelligence”) arguing that generic text wrappers and introductory slide decks are saturated while specialized clinical, laboratory and infrastructure integrations remain scarce. Plotting all 104 competitive core contracts below shows how starkly that split appears in official procurement filings: overall, 45 of 104 (43.3%) drew just one bid.

Chapter 03 Vendors & EU grants

Who keeps winning Polish public AI contracts, and what happens when EU recovery grants expire

Canonicalizing company names and stripping pasted tax IDs across award notices reveals a fragmented market overall, punctuated by a dozen specialized vendors who have built repeatable niches in public healthcare, research institutes and academic trainings.

The most frequent winner in the core dataset is Krajowy Operator Chmury Medycznej Sp. z o.o. (National Medical Cloud Operator), which signed seven hospital contracts worth 1.96M PLN across Cieszyn, Opole, Poznań (twice), Zamość, Siedlce and Legnica for cloud VoiceBots and Chatbots handling outpatient appointment lines. Not one of those seven awards faced a rival bid: four contracts worth 1.28M PLN (Cieszyn, Zamość and two stages in Poznań) were awarded sole-source under Art. 214 PZP — with hospital Section 3 filings citing the vendor's exclusive copyright and authorization to integrate voicebots with Asseco Poland's HIS AMMS hospital system — while the remaining three open tenders (Opole, Siedlce, Legnica) each drew a single bid. In hospital pharmacy software, Pharmdiver Sp. z o.o. won two single-bid clinical contracts worth 362.2k PLN in 2026 (203.0k PLN in Opole and 159.3k PLN at Rydygier Specialist Hospital in Kraków) assisting clinicians with pharmacotherapy selection.

Individual filings also document domain-specific procurements well outside generic office chat. Poland's national research network NASK-PIB paid press publisher Gremi Media S.A. (publisher of Rzeczpospolita) 356.7k PLN for licensed press text corpora to train Polish large language models (2026/BZP 00032576 ↗, Lot II). Poland's competition and consumer watchdog UOKiK awarded a 209.1k PLN contract in October 2025 (4 bids, won by Firstscore Sp. z o.o., 2025/BZP 00517087 ↗) to build a vision- and language-model (VLM/LLM) system detecting e-commerce dark patterns. Meanwhile, the state folk ensemble “Mazowsze” contracted 378.7k PLN (2025/BZP 00462052) for AI-assisted stage audio monitoring and studio transducer hardware.

As of the 3 November 2026 snapshot, 18 core proceedings (11.3%) had no published BZP award notice yet — split between 4 tenders actively open for bids ↓ in early November (Pontifical University of John Paul II in Kraków's AI & immersive lab 2026/BZP 00510375, PARP's AI-driven EDR subscription 2026/BZP 00513349, City of Łódź AI workstations 2026/BZP 00513721, and Regional Labour Office in Poznań trainings 2026/BZP 00517146) and 14 proceedings past their bid deadline awaiting result publication, including Częstochowa University of Technology's October robotics tenders (2026/BZP 00462652 for seven humanoid robots and 2026/BZP 00474618 for a research robot and manipulator).

A maintenance bill deferred to 2028

What the BZP registry establishes directly is the scale of European Union support: 70 of the 110 signed core contracts (63.6%), worth 21.10M PLN (65.5% of core contract value), are carried out within EU co-financed projects declared in Sections 2.11 or 2.14 of their notices — led by National Recovery Plan (KPO) hospital digitization grants and FERS skills programmes (filter all EU co-financed contracts ↓).

What follows from those filings is a forward-looking budget hypothesis rather than a settled outcome. KPO and structural grants cover 24- or 36-month software subscriptions, GPU racks and initial integration, but ongoing operation carries recurring telephony, cloud inference and HIS/PACS connector costs once project durability windows close around 2028–2029. On the regulatory side, obligations under the EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689) ↗ depend strictly on system classification: ordinary outpatient appointment voicebots and informational chatbots mainly face Article 50 transparency duties, whereas full high-risk compliance rules apply conditionally and — under the European Commission's regulatory framework and Digital Omnibus on AI ↗ — take effect on 2 December 2027 (Annex III use cases) and 2 August 2028 (Annex I regulated products, including clinical medical-device software). That compliance calendar converges directly with the post-grant renewal cliff: tools that measurably clear registration queues or imaging backlogs should earn permanent hospital budget lines, while grant-driven pilots risk quietly lapsing when initial licences expire.

65.5% of contract value in EU projects Measured by signed gross value, 70 EU co-financed awards account for 21.10M of 32.20M PLN (65.5%). EU funding appears in 81.6% of healthcare proceedings (31 of 38) and 83.3% of training proceedings (30 of 36).
Connecting to the national radiology hub Spring 2026 brought a wave of parallel hospital contracts — including Gliwice Oncology (2026/BZP 00195811 ↗), Opole, Siedlce and Kraków — upgrading local PACS/RIS archives to exchange imaging studies with the e-Health Centre's Intelligent Services Platform (PUI).
Chapter 04 Dirty registries

Why an unverified summary of raw BZP filings would be off by more than 11 million PLN

Putting this dataset together also served as a practical test of the gap between asking a language model to summarize a public topic and doing the data engineering required to stand behind every figure.

The BZP API does not serve a clean table of contracts. Worse, its public endpoint silently ignores the TenderId query parameter and caps batches at PageSize=500, so naive queries either truncate results or fail to link initial tender notices with later award filings. Reconstructing all 171 proceedings from 304 official notices required a deterministic Python pipeline (scripts/audit_bzp_data.py ↗) that recursively splits date windows and joins ContractNotice, amendments and TenderResultNotice records by tenderId in Python — uncovering 17 outcome notices (12 signed contracts and 5 cancellations) that surface searches miss (full methodology & audit log ↗).

Even after linking notices, summing raw BZP matches for “artificial intelligence” and “AI” would distort Polish public AI spending by over 11 million PLN for three mundane reasons. First, in veterinary institutes, AI stands for Avian Influenza diagnostic kits, while substring searches catch proper names such as catering for a preschool called “Tęczowa KrAIna”.

Second, once universities established Faculties and Departments of Artificial Intelligence, every routine purchase by those units inherited the phrase in its header — alongside multi-lot tenders where only one lot involves AI. Without CPV and lot-level auditing, “Poland's AI budget” absorbs a 4.63M PLN civil renovation of faculty rooms in Częstochowa (2026/BZP 00320127 ↗), 156k PLN in general legal counsel, office chairs and conference catering, plus non-AI lots such as renewable-energy labs and burnout courses. All 12 incidental proceedings (5.22M PLN across 11 signed contracts) remain in the JSON file for auditability, excluded by default and available via a single toggle below (include & inspect all 12 incidental tenders ↓).

Third, official award notices contain human clerical errors in Section 8.2. Cross-checking Section 6.4 winning bids against Section 8.2 contract values caught two major distortions totaling 5.83M PLN: at the e-Health Centre (2026/BZP 00009631 ↗), an extra trailing zero turned a 544,000 PLN training award into 5,440,000 PLN; at Łukasiewicz–EMAG in Katowice (2024/BZP 00014936 ↗), the authority pasted the combined three-lot contract total of 468,507 PLN into Section 8.2 of every single lot, inflating the filing to 1,405,521 PLN (+937k PLN).

Verification pipeline 1. Harvest ezamowienia.gov.pl API with recursive <500 window splitting (01.01.2024 – 03.11.2026).
2. Strip veterinary (Avian Influenza) and word-boundary collisions.
3. Join 304 notices into 171 proceedings by tenderId in Python (bypassing BZP's broken TenderId filter).
4. Cross-check HTML Sections 2.11/2.14, 4.x, 6.1, 6.4 and 8.2; isolate AI lots in multi-topic tenders.
5. Validate every figure via automated gate verify_bzp_report.py.

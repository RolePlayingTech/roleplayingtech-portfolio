ISSUE 01 · INVESTIGATION & DATASET Polish Public Procurement (BZP 2024–2026) Published: 3 November 2026 · ~9 min read

What do Polish public institutions actually buy under the ‘AI’ label?

As Poland's national AI oversight rules came into effect in late October 2026, public debate split along familiar lines: official optimism about a digital leap on one side, and jokes that public-sector AI begins and ends with two-day ChatGPT workshops on the other.

Instead of arguing from headlines, I pulled 287 official notices from Poland's Public Procurement Bulletin API covering 2024–2026, linked them by proceeding ID, stripped out false positives and clerical errors, and audited every signed contract.

Read first · Scope of this dataset
  • What BZP covers: Poland's Public Procurement Bulletin (BZP) records domestic public tenders below EU publication thresholds (typically from 130,000 PLN net up to roughly 0.6–0.9M PLN for supplies and services, and higher for construction or social services).
  • What sits outside it: Multi-million-zloty central ministry tenders published in the EU's TED journal form a separate tier. BZP captures everyday reality on the ground: regional hospitals, technical universities, county offices and labour agencies.
  • Data hygiene: I removed false matches (such as veterinary tests for avian influenza, abbreviated AI), isolated building renovations and catering at university departments with “AI” in their name, and fixed a 10× clerical zero error in one award notice.

Chapter 01 The headline illusion

Why public AI looks like endless prompt workshops on the surface, while the money goes to hospitals and labs

Scroll through raw tender titles in the Public Procurement Bulletin without opening the award protocols, and it is easy to conclude that Polish institutions mostly buy introductory AI workshops. Courses for regional civil servants, teacher trainings and two-day generative AI seminars appear almost every week. In my cleaned dataset, they account for 36 of the 159 core proceedings, or 22.6 per cent of all procurement attempts (inspect all 36 training tenders below ↓).

That impression flips as soon as you add up the signed contract values instead of counting notice titles. The median training contract in BZP is worth 36,531 PLN gross (around €8,500), and several workshops closed below 2,000 PLN. All 21 awarded training contracts across nearly three years add up to 2.06M PLN, just 6.5 per cent of core spending.

More than nine out of every ten zlotys flow into two very different places: hospital IT departments and university research laboratories.

The largest bucket is operational software and clinical or administrative integrations (15.72M PLN across 42 signed contracts, median 268.8k PLN). Reading the specifications chronologically from 2024 to late 2026 shows a clear shift away from generic chat toys towards narrow operational tools. Within a single week of March 2026, the Children's Memorial Health Institute in Warsaw signed two separate contracts: 365k PLN with Siemens Healthcare for AI-assisted medical imaging software, and 916.5k PLN with Orange Polska for five interactive AI reception avatars. The Regional Children's Rehabilitation Hospital in Ameryka near Olsztynek contracted 1.28M PLN for hardware and AI tools supporting clinical documentation and patient phone scheduling. Meanwhile, Łowicz County awarded a 680.7k PLN contract in August 2026 for a municipal task workflow and intranet RAG chatbot grounded strictly in internal county records (filter all software deployments ↓).

The second pillar is GPU compute servers, diagnostic gear and lab robotics (13.30M PLN across 27 contracts, median 199k PLN). Technical faculties, national institutes and regional branches of the National Health Fund (NFZ) are buying multi-GPU servers for local LLM inference, automated fluorescence microscopes (620k PLN at the National Marine Fisheries Research Institute in Gdynia), AI-assisted ultrasound platforms (199k PLN at the hospital in Nowa Sól) and humanoid research robots in Częstochowa and Poznań. The single largest core contract in the dataset, worth 4.94M PLN, was signed in March 2026 by Jan Długosz University in Częstochowa to equip a new Laboratory for Artificial Intelligence in Diagnostic Imaging (filter hardware & lab contracts ↓).

Who spends the most Universities and research institutes account for 15.96M PLN (45 contracts), followed closely by hospitals and healthcare providers at 10.38M PLN (27 contracts). Together they represent 82.4% of signed BZP spend.
Silesia matches Warsaw's region Below EU thresholds, Warsaw has no monopoly. The Silesian voivodeship (Częstochowa, Katowice, Gliwice, Cieszyn) generated 9.82M PLN in signed contracts, virtually tied with Mazovia's 9.84M PLN (view Silesia in the explorer ↓).
Chapter 02 The bidding divide

Twenty-three bidders for a slide deck, one bidder for a hospital server

The most revealing column in the dataset turned out not to be contract price, but the number of bids submitted per tender. It draws a sharp line between the crowded surface of Poland's AI market and its underserved engineering floor.

When Kielce University of Technology posted tender 2026/BZP 00465518 in autumn 2026 for a staff training course worth 7,200 PLN, 23 separate vendors competed for the job. An online AI training tender for Lower Silesian civil servants worth 29,750 PLN drew 22 bids, and a 1,750 PLN workshop for teachers in Lubusz attracted 14 bidders. Across the entire training category, the median tender receives seven competing offers (sort trainings by bid count ↓).

Step down into clinical software integrations, hospital pharmacy decision support, PACS imaging archives or on-premise security deployments, and the crowd vanishes. Across signed AI software deployments, the median bid count drops to one. Out of 38 awarded software contracts that reported bid numbers, 26 (68.4%) had only a single bidder, leaving the public buyer with zero price competition (view all 26 single-bid software contracts ↓).

Some buyers find nobody at all, or receive quotes far above budget. In March 2026, the 5th Military Clinical Hospital in Kraków had to cancel tender 2026/BZP 00155059 for laboratory diagnostics AI software after zero bids arrived by the deadline. Overall, 27 of the 171 proceedings ended in cancellation, frequently on first attempts to procure GPU servers or specialized medical integrations.

Earlier this year I wrote an essay (in Polish, “Blue Oceans of Artificial Intelligence”) arguing that the middle of the AI map, generic text generation, chat wrappers and introductory slide decks, is saturated, while the real shortage sits where software meets clinics, lab benches and legacy infrastructure. Plotting all 93 signed contracts below turns that observation into hard procurement numbers.

Chapter 03 Vendors & EU grants

Who keeps winning Polish public AI contracts, and what happens when EU recovery grants expire

Normalizing company names across award notices shows a fragmented market overall, punctuated by a handful of specialized vendors who have quietly built repeatable niches in public healthcare and research.

The most frequent winner in the entire dataset is Krajowy Operator Chmury Medycznej Sp. z o.o. (National Medical Cloud Operator), which signed six hospital contracts worth 1.81M PLN across Poznań, Zamość, Legnica, Opole and Cieszyn for cloud VoiceBots and Chatbots handling outpatient appointment lines. In hospital pharmacy software, Pharmdiver won two single-bid clinical contracts in 2026 (at the University Clinical Hospital in Opole for 203k PLN and Rydygier Specialist Hospital in Kraków for 159k PLN) to assist doctors and pharmacists with drug selection.

Individual filings also capture unusual projects beyond routine back-office automation. In January 2026, Poland's national research network NASK-PIB paid press publisher Gremi Media S.A. (publisher of the daily Rzeczpospolita) 356.7k PLN for licensed text corpora to train Polish large language models (2026/BZP 00032576). In Wrocław, the regional branch of the National Health Fund spent 622.4k PLN expanding its server cluster to host an internal LLM, while the state folk ensemble “Mazowsze” contracted a 378.7k PLN AI-assisted educational media platform. Among open proceedings awaiting award, Poland's competition watchdog UOKiK is procuring a vision-language model (VLM) system (2025/BZP 00411633), and Częstochowa University of Technology is buying seven additional humanoid robots.

A maintenance bill deferred to 2028

One structural thread runs through most of these purchases: European Union funding. Exactly 60 of the 99 signed core contracts (60.6%), representing 20.26M PLN, declare EU co-financing in Section 2.11 of their notices, led by National Recovery Plan (KPO) hospital digitization grants and FERS skills programmes (filter all 60 EU co-financed contracts ↓).

Grants cover 24- or 36-month software licences, GPU racks and initial deployment smoothly. The real stress test arrives in 2028–2029 once project durability windows close. VoiceBots, LLM servers and PACS integrations carry ongoing variable costs: cloud inference and telephony usage, connector maintenance whenever legacy hospital software updates, and compliance oversight under the new regulatory regime. Tools that genuinely clear patient registration queues or radiology backlogs will earn a line item in hospital operating budgets. Systems bought mainly because grant money was available risk quietly going dark when the initial licence expires.

63.4% of money tied to EU funds Measured by contract value rather than tender count, EU co-financed awards represent nearly two-thirds of all core BZP spending (20.26M of 31.97M PLN).
Connecting to the national radiology hub Spring 2026 brought a wave of parallel hospital contracts (Opole, Gliwice, Siedlce) upgrading local PACS/RIS imaging archives to exchange studies with the e-Health Centre's national Intelligent Services Platform.
Chapter 04 Dirty registries

Why an automated AI summary of raw registry data would be wrong by over 10 million PLN

Putting this dataset together also served as a practical test of something that rarely makes it into AI demos: the gap between asking a language model to summarize a public topic and doing the engineering work required to stand behind every figure.

The BZP API does not hand out a clean table of contracts. It serves separate HTML notices (ContractNotice, amendments and TenderResultNotice) that must be linked by tenderId and parsed section by section. Asking an unverified script or chatbot to pull and sum notices matching “artificial intelligence” and “AI” would inflate Polish public AI spending by over ten million zlotys for three mundane reasons.

First, in veterinary institutes, the abbreviation AI stands for Avian Influenza (bird flu) diagnostic kits. Full-text search also catches substring collisions in Polish proper names, such as preschool catering for a kindergarten named “Tęczowa KrAIna”.

Second, once Polish universities established Faculties and Departments of Artificial Intelligence, every routine purchase by those units inherited the phrase in its header. Without filtering by CPV procurement codes and actual scope, “Poland's AI budget” absorbs a 4.63M PLN civil construction renovation of faculty rooms, 156k PLN in general legal counsel, swivel chairs, student travel and conference catering. Those 12 proceedings (5.20M PLN in signed contracts) are kept in the JSON file for transparency, excluded by default, and available via a single toggle below (include & inspect all 12 incidental tenders ↓).

Third, official procurement forms contain plain human typos. Cross-checking estimated values against winning bids and final contract fields flagged notice 2026/BZP 00009631 from the e-Health Centre (CeZ). Two sections of the filing show roughly half a million PLN; the third adds an accidental trailing zero, turning 544,000 PLN into 5.44 million PLN. Left unchecked, that single keystroke would have nearly tripled Poland's reported spending on AI training.

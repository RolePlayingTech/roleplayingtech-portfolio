ISSUE 01 · INVESTIGATION & DATASET Polish Public Procurement (BZP 2024–2026) Snapshot: 3 November 2026 · ~12 min read

What do Polish public institutions actually buy under the ‘AI’ label?

As Poland's national AI oversight rules came into effect in late October 2026, public debate split along familiar lines: official optimism about a digital leap on one side, and jokes that public-sector AI begins and ends with two-day ChatGPT workshops on the other.

Instead of arguing from headlines, I pulled 304 linked notices from Poland's Public Procurement Bulletin API (2024–2026), reconstructed 171 unique proceedings by tenderId, read their full HTML protocols — buyer cost estimates, evaluation criteria, bid rejections and cancellation grounds (exposing 60.7% effective zero-rival spend, 12 of 12 HIS/PACS hospital lock-in and 31 collapsed tenders) — and summed the full signed gross contract values across all 110 core contracts (often 12–36-month multi-year contracts rather than single-year cash outlays).

Chapter 01 The headline illusion

Why public AI looks like endless prompt workshops on the surface, while the money goes to hospitals and labs

Scroll through raw tender titles in the Public Procurement Bulletin without opening the award protocols, and it is easy to conclude that Polish institutions mostly buy introductory AI workshops. Courses for regional civil servants, teacher trainings and two-day generative AI seminars appear almost every week. In the audited dataset, they account for 36 of the 159 core proceedings, or 22.6 per cent of all procurement attempts (inspect all 36 training tenders below ↓).

That impression flips as soon as you sum the signed gross contract values instead of counting notice titles. The median training contract in BZP is worth 47,000 PLN gross (around €11,000), and small individual lots closed below 2,000 PLN (1,482 PLN for a teaching workshop lot at the Polish Naval Academy in Gdynia; 1,750 PLN in Lubusz). All 26 signed training contracts across nearly three years add up to 2.46M PLN — just 7.6 per cent of core contract value. Another 9 research and advisory contracts add 0.82M PLN (2.6%, median 51.0k PLN).

Nearly nine out of every ten contracted zlotys — 89.8 per cent (28.92M PLN across 75 signed contracts from 110 proceedings) — go into two very different places: hospital IT departments and university research laboratories.

The largest bucket is AI software and operational deployments (17.17M PLN across 45 signed contracts from 62 proceedings, 53.3% of core value, median 255.3k PLN). Reading specifications chronologically from 2024 to November 2026 shows a shift from generic chat pilots toward narrow clinical and administrative systems. In February 2026 (award notices filed in early March), the Children's Memorial Health Institute in Warsaw signed two separate contracts: 365.3k PLN with Siemens Healthcare for AI-assisted MRI software, and 916.5k PLN with Orange Polska for interactive AI reception avatars (2026/BZP 00137744 ↗). The Regional Children's Rehabilitation Hospital in Ameryka near Olsztynek contracted a 1.28M PLN six-lot KPO package (2026/BZP 00124082 ↗) led by clinical documentation AI (490.8k PLN) and a virtual phone receptionist (165.0k PLN) alongside a dedicated AI server (471.3k PLN) and IT admin training. Meanwhile, Łowicz County signed a 680.7k PLN lump-sum contract with Agentic P.S.A. (2026/BZP 00379906 ↗, 5 bids) bundling a broader municipal Task Management System, Intranet portal, and staff trainings with a RAG chatbot designed to retrieve answers from internal county documents (filter all 62 software proceedings ↓).

The second pillar is GPU compute servers, diagnostic gear and lab robotics (11.74M PLN across 30 signed contracts from 48 proceedings, 36.5% of core value, median 171.4k PLN). Technical faculties, national institutes and regional branches of the National Health Fund (NFZ) are buying multi-GPU servers for local LLM inference, automated fluorescence microscopes (620.0k PLN at MIR-PIB in Gdynia), AI-assisted clinical ultrasound systems (332.0k PLN in Sochaczew, 199.0k PLN in Nowa Sól) and humanoid research robots in Częstochowa and Poznań (filter hardware & lab contracts ↓).

Because public buyers sometimes bundle AI into larger turnkey contracts, every core record carries an explicit scope tag. Pure-scope AI procurements account for 97 signed contracts worth 19.63M PLN across 139 proceedings (61.0% of core value, median 154.2k PLN; filter pure-AI contracts ↓). The remaining 13 mixed-scope contracts total 12.57M PLN across 20 proceedings (39.0%), where an inseparable lump sum or hybrid KPO package combines AI with construction works, heavy clinical equipment, physical services or general enterprise IT. The single largest core contract in the dataset — 4.94M PLN at Jan Długosz University in Częstochowa (2026/BZP 00163288 ↗) — is a design-and-build contract bundling architectural works, room construction and a CT scanner for its new Laboratory for AI in Diagnostic Imaging; the second-largest mixed contract, 2.16M PLN at Łukasiewicz–WIT in Warsaw (2025/BZP 00543355 ↗), combines 37 months of physical security guard services with AI-assisted access control.

Grouped by BZP notice publication year, signed core contract value rose 4.8×: from 4.05M PLN in 2024 (18 contracts across 24 proceedings) to 8.56M PLN in 2025 (35 across 41) and 19.59M PLN across the first ten months of 2026 (57 signed contracts across 94 proceedings to 3 November). Measured by physical contract-signing date instead — shifting three awards published in January 2024 (611.4k PLN) back to December 2023 — annual totals move from 0.61M PLN (Dec 2023, 3 contracts) and 3.44M PLN (2024, 15) to 8.95M PLN (2025, 37) and 19.19M PLN (2026, 55), a 5.6× jump between 2024 and 2026.

Who contracts the most Universities and research institutes lead with 15.24M PLN (47 signed contracts across 71 proceedings, 47.3%), followed by hospitals and healthcare providers at 11.36M PLN (31 contracts across 38 proceedings, 35.3%). Together they account for 26.60M PLN (82.6%) across 78 contracts — ahead of local governments and labour offices (2.70M PLN, 21 contracts), central agencies (2.70M PLN, 8) and schools (0.21M PLN, 3).
Silesia holds a strong #2 behind Masovia After fixing a 3× repeated Section 8.2 clerical total at Katowice's EMAG institute and isolating the AI lab lot at a school in Pawłowice, Masovia leads with 10.69M PLN (30 contracts, 33.2%) and Silesia stands a strong second at 8.66M PLN (14 contracts, 26.9% of the national total — well ahead of Lower Silesia's 2.00M PLN and Opole's 1.73M PLN), showing there is no Warsaw monopoly thanks to universities and hospitals in Częstochowa, Gliwice and Katowice (view Silesia in the explorer ↓).
Chapter 02 The bidding divide

Twenty-three bidders for a workshop, zero rivals for up to 60.7% of the money

Counting tenders one by one understates how thin competition is in Polish public-sector AI, because rival bidders pack into small training lots while large operational contracts go uncontested. By contract count, single-bid open tenders make up 43.3 per cent (45 of 104 competitive awards) — yet weighted by contract value they absorb 54.1 per cent of all competitive spending (16.71M of 30.88M PLN, or 51.9% of core value). Add the 6 sole-source contracts filed without a Section 6.1 bid count under Art. 305 / Art. 214 PZP (1.33M PLN, 4.1% of value; inspect all 6 sole-source awards ↓), and 51 of the 110 signed core contracts (46.4%) worth 18.04M PLN — 56.0 per cent of the entire 32.20M PLN dataset — went to vendors facing zero rival bids. At the opposite pole, 35 crowded tenders attracting 4 to 23 bids (nearly one-third of all signed contracts, 31.8%) split a combined total of just 3.99M PLN, or 12.4 per cent of contracted money, while the middle 24 contracts with 2–3 bids account for 10.18M PLN (31.6%).

Auditing the full HTML text of Sections 3.1 and 6.1.6 across every award notice shows that even that 56.0% baseline understates effective concentration. First, four of the 45 awards recorded with 1 bid in Section 6.1 (worth 2.13M PLN: Pixel Technology's 922.8k PLN PACS/PUI integration and KOChM's 453.4k PLN voicebot in Opole [2026/BZP 00297319 ↗], upmedic's 528.9k PLN clinical LLM structuring at Poznań University of Medical Sciences [2026/BZP 00172477 ↗], and StatSoft's 227.5k PLN licences at Poznań University of Life Sciences) were statutory sole-source contracts under Art. 305 PZP where clerks typed the lone invited supplier as one bid — bringing true statutory sole-source awards to 10 of 110 contracts (9.1%) worth 3.46M PLN (10.7% of spend) alongside 41 open single-bid contracts (14.58M PLN). Section 3.1.1 justifications stretch from hospital HIS copyright down to a 6,150 PLN two-day AI workshop for school principals in Zgierz (2024/BZP 00620147 ↗), justified in a single sentence declaring the trainer “a specialist in the field of AI of high renown, making him a unique and irreplaceable personality” („jest specjalistą w dziedzinie AI o wysokiej renomie, co czyni go osobowością jedyną i niepowtarzalną”). Second, buyers rejected 121 of 460 submitted bids (26.3% — over one in four offers), including 46 (10.0%) disqualified for abnormally low price (Section 6.1.7). In 8 multi-bid tenders worth 1.50M PLN, buyers disqualified every rival bidder, leaving a single surviving offer — including the Katowice Oncology Center's screening mammography AI (2025/BZP 00564260 ↗, signed at 424.5k PLN after rejecting a 238.8k PLN competing offer as abnormally low) and two humanoid-robot deliveries at Częstochowa University of Technology (549.9k PLN and 90.0k PLN). Including those eight awards, 53 of 104 contracts with reported bid counts (51.0%) — and 59 of 110 signed core contracts (53.6%) worth 19.54M PLN, or 60.7 per cent of all public AI money — faced zero surviving competitors at final evaluation.

The crowded end of that market is visible in almost every general training tender. When Kielce University of Technology tendered a three-lot staff AI training worth 7,200 PLN in September 2026 (2026/BZP 00465518 ↗), its individual lots drew 23, 15 and 22 competing bids — a peak of 23 bidders on a single lot. (Because the same training firms routinely bid on parallel lots, the dataset records the maximum bid count on any single AI lot rather than summing bids across lots; non-AI lots in multi-topic tenders were likewise stripped out, such as removing burnout and Norma Pro courses from Lower Silesia's 2026/BZP 00304575 to leave AI Lot 2 at 16,684 PLN and 10 bids, or excluding a renewable-energy lab in Pawłowice's 2025/BZP 00133931 to leave the AI lab lot at 143.7k PLN and 6 bids.) An online AI course for Lower Silesian civil servants worth 29,750 PLN (2026/BZP 00428993) drew 22 bids, and a 1,750 PLN workshop in Lubusz attracted 14 bidders. Across all 24 competitive training contracts with disclosed bid counts, the median tender received six offers, and only 3 of 24 (12.5%) had a single bidder (sort trainings by bid count ↓).

Move into clinical software integrations, hospital pharmacy decision support, PACS imaging archives or on-premise security deployments, and concentration becomes stark. Across competitive AI software deployments, the median bid count drops to one: 27 of 41 contracts (65.9%, worth 12.09M PLN) attracted only a single bidder (view all 27 single-bid software contracts ↓). Combined with 4 sole-source hospital contracts (1.28M PLN), 31 of all 45 signed software contracts (68.9%) and 13.37M of 17.17M PLN — 77.8 per cent of all AI software spending — faced zero price competition. The contrast inside that category underscores what separates an open tender from a locked-in one: when the Kraków Academy of Fine Arts tendered a standalone admissions chatbot with no legacy back-office integration (2026/BZP 00450838 ↗, 74.8k PLN), 19 vendors competed — whereas integrating an existing hospital HIS or PACS archive, such as the Gliwice branch of the National Institute of Oncology connecting its Agfa imaging system to the national Intelligent Services Platform (2026/BZP 00195811 ↗, 485.9k PLN), draws just a single bidder.

The 170,000 PLN competition cliff

Ranking all 104 competitive contracts by gross value exposes an exact 50/50 fault line at 170,000 PLN gross. In the lower half — 52 contracts below 170,000 PLN totaling 3.43M PLN, or just 11.1 per cent of competitive value — median competition stands at 4.5 bids (4 bids below 50k PLN and 5 bids across 50k–170k PLN), and only 11 of 52 contracts (21.2%) end with a single bidder. Cross 170,000 PLN gross, and the market flips: across the upper 52 contracts worth 27.45M PLN (88.9% of competitive value), median bids drop vertically to 1, and 34 of 52 contracts (65.4%, worth 15.82M PLN) attract a single bidder (67.6%, or 25 of 37, across 170k–500k PLN, and 60.0%, or 9 of 15, at 500k+ PLN).

Regulatory irony since 1 January 2026

Raising Poland's mandatory PZP procurement threshold from 130,000 to 170,000 PLN net (roughly 209,000 PLN gross at standard VAT) removes from mandatory BZP visibility precisely that lower, multi-bid half of the market — leaving behind in the public bulletin the upper tier where single-bid awards are already the norm.

What zero rivals cost, 13× bid spreads, and tenders with zero quality criteria

Comparing buyers' pre-tender net cost estimates in ContractNotice Section 4.3 against signed gross awards in Section 8.2 puts a direct price tag on competition. Across homogeneous 23%-VAT software and hardware contracts with clean single-scope estimates (n = 31), when only 1 valid bid survives (n = 18), the median signed gross contract lands at 1.230× the net estimate — meaning the institution pays 100.0% of its pre-tender net valuation (0% discount) plus 23% VAT. With 2–3 surviving bids (n = 9), the median drops to 93.8% of the net estimate (1.153× gross/net); with 4 or more surviving bids (n = 4), it falls to 54.0% of the net estimate (0.664× gross/net — a 46.0% net discount). Across all 45 single-lot VAT-clean contracts, median gross-to-net pricing compresses from 1.230× (1 bid) to 0.758× (4+ submitted bids): Kraków Academy of Fine Arts estimated its candidate chatbot (2026/BZP 00450838) at 177.5k PLN net and signed at 74.8k PLN gross (34.3% of net estimate) across 19 bids, whereas University Clinical Hospital in Opole estimated its single-bid drug-selection AI (2026/BZP 00201117 ↗) at 111.5k PLN net and signed at 203.0k PLN gross.

Sections 6.2 and 6.3 also expose extreme valuation uncertainty in custom software — paired with a near-absence of quality screening. On GPU servers and lab hardware, rival quotes cluster tightly around wholesale component costs, with a median highest-to-lowest valid bid ratio of 1.12× (maximum 1.39×). In AI software, valid bids with zero rejections diverge by a median of 2.71× and up to 13.25× — from 165.0k PLN to 2.19M PLN across three valid bids for Ameryka Children's Hospital's virtual phone receptionist (2026/BZP 00124082, Lot 3) and 9.13× (269.4k PLN vs 2.46M PLN) for UMCS Lublin's AI training-data platform (2025/BZP 00601774 ↗) — while general trainings hit 35.27× (2,240 PLN vs 79,000 PLN at Kielce University of Technology). Yet 63.5 per cent of competitive AI tenders (94 of 148) — and 72.2 per cent in AI software (39 of 54) — include zero substantive quality criteria (32.4% evaluate 100% price, another 31.1% add only formal warranty, SLA or delivery-time checkboxes, and 83.1% weight price at ≥ 60%). Out of all 100 statutory competitive signed contracts, substantive quality unambiguously beat a cheaper valid bid only twice (2.0%) — both labour-market research studies at regional labour offices in Warsaw and Łódź, and zero times in AI software or hardware.

Hard HIS/PACS lock-in and the two-speed KPO hospital sprint

Breaking outcomes down by buyer type, bidding calendars and named clinical systems shows where the bottleneck sits. Local governments and regional labour offices see single-bid outcomes in just 2 of 21 signed contracts (9.5%) (inspect those 21 local government awards ↓), universities and research institutes in 19 of 46 competitive awards (41.3%, or 20 of 47 including sole-source), and central agencies in 4 of 8 (50.0%) — alongside the highest cancellation rate of any sector at 38.5 per cent of concluded tenders (5 of 13, or 31.3% across all 16 proceedings). At the opposite extreme sit hospitals and healthcare providers, where 20 of 27 competitive tenders (74.1%) drew a single bid (inspect all 20 single-bid hospital awards ↓), and 24 of 31 signed contracts (77.4%, worth 8.19M PLN) including sole-source awards went uncontested.

Across all 148 competitive notices, the median initial bidding window is just 9.0 calendar days (41.2% give ≤ 8 days, hugging the Art. 283 PZP 7-day floor), whereas buyers take a median of 28.0 days (3.1× longer; 39.0 days total from notice to signature, and 86.0 days across retried chains) to evaluate and sign. In custom AI software worth ≥ 170k PLN, every multi-bid award (7 of 7, 100%) had its deadline extended (final median 16.0 days). In hospitals (64.0% extended, 12.0-day median), however, extra days cannot break proprietary lock-in: the National Institute of Cardiology (2026/BZP 00213382 ↗, 253.5k PLN) extended its CGM CliniNET VoiceBot deadline five times from 12 to 27 days and still received one bid. Across healthcare, every tender naming an incumbent clinical or IT system (AMMS/Asseco, CliniNET, AGFA EI, Pixel, CeZ PUI) ended with zero rivals (12 of 12 signed awards = 100.0%, plus 4 cancellations; across all AI software, 16 of 17 = 94.1% vs 53.6% in greenfield tools) — and the Provincial Specialist Hospital in Legnica (2025/BZP 00478008 ↗, 70.0k PLN) explicitly required in Section 5.4 that “the Contractor cooperate with Asseco Poland S.A. or hold its authorization” (InfoMedica/AMMS). Across 7 hospital tenders to connect radiology archives to the e-Health Centre's Intelligent Services Platform (PUI), 0 of 7 drew two bids, 4 of 7 collapsed, and the 3 signed awards varied 3.2× in price (285.4k PLN in Siedlce, 485.9k PLN in Gliwice, 922.8k PLN sole-source in Opole).

That split also resolves an apparent paradox in EU funding. Pooled across the whole dataset, EU co-financed contracts show a slightly lower single-bid rate (41.8%, 28 of 67 competitive awards) than domestically funded contracts (45.9%, 17 of 37), while mixed-scope turnkey packages (aiScope: mixed) hit 61.5 per cent single-bid (8 of 13) versus 40.7 per cent (37 of 91) in pure AI. Disaggregating EU programmes by quarter reveals two opposite EU streams cancelling each other out: multi-year FERS skill grants (6–23 bids per workshop) mask the Spring 2026 KPO D1.1.2 hospital sprint. In H1 2026 alone, hospitals crammed 26 of 38 (68.4%) of their entire 34-month AI procurement (17 notices in March–April 2026), signing 19 contracts worth 8.45M PLN (74.4% of healthcare AI spend) at an 84.2 per cent (16 of 19) zero-rival rate. Once that mid-2026 KPO window shut, hospital AI notices dropped to 1 in Q3 2026 (0 KPO) — and Poland's economy-wide single-bid rate plunged from 64.7% (Q1) and 55.0% (Q2) to 0.0 per cent (0 of 11 contracts) in Q3 2026.

Autopsy of the 31 cancelled tenders — why one in five AI purchases collapses

Reading Section 5.2 across all 31 cancelled core proceedings (22.0% of 141 concluded core tenders, or 19.5% of all 159; 32 across all 171; show all 31 ↓) overturns the guess that public AI tenders fail mainly for lack of vendor interest. Only 6 of 31 cancellations (19.4%) had zero bids (Art. 255(1) PZP, such as the 5th Military Clinical Hospital in Kraków's laboratory AI tender 2026/BZP 00155059). In 24 of 31 failures (77.4%), suppliers wanted the contract and submitted at least 53 bids — yet proceedings collapsed from budget shock (14 of 31 = 45.2%, Art. 255(3)) or 100% bid disqualification (8 of 31 = 25.8%, Art. 255(2)). Budget shock killed NAWA's chatbot advisory tender (2025/BZP 00126499 ↗, lone 95.9k PLN quote at 3.2× its 30k PLN budget) and FRSE's first Erasmus+ chatbot tender (2025/BZP 00350395 ↗, where rejecting three cheaper offers left a 393.6k PLN bid against a 234.5k PLN cap before Retry #2 drew 6 valid bids and signed at 220.0k PLN). Formal disqualifications killed a 2.6k PLN Azure AI teacher course over an unsigned .docx, led Kociewskie Health Center to twice reject its lone bidder over bid-day liability insurance wording, and saw Poznań University of Medical Sciences reject upmedic's lone open bid (2026/BZP 00146627 ↗) only to award 528.9k PLN sole-source to the exact same upmedic 14 days later (2026/BZP 00172477 ↗).

The remaining 3 of 31 cancellations (9.7%) collapsed over procedural defects or failed negotiations (Art. 255(6) & (8)): the Regional Labour Office in Łódź (2024/BZP 00424907 ↗) cancelled a study despite 8 valid bids after discovering a bidder's partner was WUP's former Deputy Director and ex-tender-committee chair, while the 4th Military Clinical Hospital in Wrocław (2026/BZP 00180494 ↗) failed to agree terms even in 1-on-1 sole-source PUI talks. Across 26 distinct failed initiatives, half (13 of 26; 3 pending) eventually signed on retry, while 10 of 26 (38.5%) were abandoned completely. Chapter 03 Vendors & EU grants

Who keeps winning Polish public AI contracts, and what happens when EU recovery grants expire

Canonicalizing company names and stripping pasted tax IDs across award notices reveals a fragmented market overall, punctuated by a dozen specialized vendors who have built repeatable niches in public healthcare, research institutes and academic trainings.

The most frequent winner in the core dataset is Krajowy Operator Chmury Medycznej Sp. z o.o. (National Medical Cloud Operator), which signed seven hospital contracts worth 1.96M PLN across Cieszyn, Opole, Poznań (twice), Zamość, Siedlce and Legnica for cloud VoiceBots and Chatbots handling outpatient appointment lines — making it the #1 supplier in Poland across pure-AI contracts (10.0% of the entire 19.63M PLN pure-AI market). Across the dataset, the top three pure-AI suppliers (KOChM, Stinet and Orange Polska) won 11 of 11 contracts (100%) with zero rival bidders. Auditing Section 3.1 across KOChM's seven awards shows that five contracts worth 1.74M PLN were statutory sole-source awards under Art. 305 / Art. 214 PZP (four filed without Section 6.1 worth 1.28M PLN in Cieszyn, Zamość and twice in Poznań, plus Opole's 2026/BZP 00297319 ↗ worth 453.4k PLN where the lone invited vendor was entered as 1 bid), citing exclusive copyright and authorization to integrate voicebots with Asseco Poland's HIS AMMS — while the remaining two open tenders (Siedlce on a 7-day window; Legnica requiring Asseco authorization) each drew one bid. That lock-in carries a measurable renewal price ratchet: at the Poznań Mother and Child Hospital, KOChM's 12-month sole-source voicebot renewal (2024/BZP 00574495 ↗ → 2026/BZP 00514632 ↗) jumped +16.1% from 219.9k PLN to 255.3k PLN (21.3k PLN/month). In hospital pharmacy software, Pharmdiver Sp. z o.o. won two single-bid clinical contracts worth 362.2k PLN in 2026 (203.0k PLN in Opole and 159.3k PLN at Rydygier Specialist Hospital in Kraków) assisting clinicians with pharmacotherapy selection.

Individual filings also document domain-specific procurements well outside generic office chat. Poland's national research network NASK-PIB paid press publisher Gremi Media S.A. (publisher of Rzeczpospolita) 356.7k PLN for licensed press text corpora to train Polish large language models (2026/BZP 00032576 ↗, Lot II). In Wrocław, the Lower Silesian branch of the National Health Fund (NFZ) committed 622.4k PLN to expand its server environment for LLM deployment, while the state folk ensemble “Mazowsze” contracted 378.7k PLN (2025/BZP 00462052) for AI-assisted stage audio monitoring and studio transducer hardware. Poland's competition and consumer watchdog UOKiK also awarded a 209.1k PLN contract in October 2025 (4 bids, won by Firstscore Sp. z o.o., 2025/BZP 00517087 ↗) to build a vision- and language-model (VLM/LLM) system detecting e-commerce dark patterns.

Auditing Section 7.2 enterprise-size flags across all 110 signed contracts (and verifying sole traders and micro-firms missing checkboxes) also overturns the assumption that public IT money flows mainly to giant corporate integrators: micro, small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) won 105 of 110 contracts (95.5%) and 30.21M PLN (93.8% of all spend — staying above 92% even at ≥ 170k PLN and ≥ 500k PLN). Confirmed large enterprises won just 4 contracts totaling 1.82M PLN (5.6%) — Orange Polska (916.5k PLN), WASKO (467.4k PLN), Siemens Healthcare (365.3k PLN) and ACTION (69.4k PLN), plus one educational foundation (179.3k PLN, 0.6%) — while public universities and research institutes, which spent 15.24M PLN as buyers, won 0 of 110 contracts (0.0%) as suppliers, even in research studies and staff trainings.

A maintenance bill deferred to 2028

What the BZP registry establishes directly is the scale of European Union support: 70 of the 110 signed core contracts (63.6%), worth 21.10M PLN (65.5% of core contract value), are carried out within EU co-financed projects declared in Sections 2.11 or 2.14 of their notices — led by National Recovery Plan (KPO) hospital digitization grants and FERS skills programmes (filter all EU co-financed contracts ↓).

What follows from those filings is a forward-looking budget hypothesis rather than a settled outcome. KPO and structural grants cover 24- or 36-month software subscriptions, GPU racks and initial integration, but ongoing operation carries recurring telephony, cloud inference and HIS/PACS connector costs once project durability windows close around 2028–2029. On the regulatory side, obligations under the EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689) ↗ depend strictly on system classification: ordinary outpatient appointment voicebots and informational chatbots mainly face Article 50 transparency duties, whereas full high-risk compliance rules apply conditionally and — under the European Commission's regulatory framework and Digital Omnibus on AI ↗ — take effect on 2 December 2027 (Annex III use cases) and 2 August 2028 (Annex I regulated products, including clinical medical-device software). That compliance calendar converges directly with the post-grant renewal cliff: tools that measurably clear registration queues or imaging backlogs should earn permanent hospital budget lines, while grant-driven pilots risk quietly lapsing when initial licences expire.

Chapter 04 Dirty registries

Why an unverified summary of raw BZP filings would be off by more than 11 million PLN

Putting this dataset together also served as a practical test of the gap between asking a language model to summarize a public topic and doing the data engineering required to stand behind every figure.

The BZP API does not serve a clean table of contracts. Worse, its public endpoint silently ignores the TenderId query parameter and caps batches at PageSize=500, so naive queries either truncate results or fail to link initial tender notices with later award filings. Reconstructing all 171 proceedings from 304 official notices required a deterministic Python pipeline (scripts/audit_bzp_data.py ↗) that recursively splits date windows and joins ContractNotice, amendments and TenderResultNotice records by tenderId in Python — uncovering 17 outcome notices (12 signed contracts and 5 cancellations) that surface searches miss (full methodology & audit log ↗).

Even after linking notices, summing raw BZP matches for “artificial intelligence” and “AI” would distort Polish public AI spending by over 11 million PLN for three mundane reasons. First, in veterinary institutes, AI stands for Avian Influenza diagnostic kits, while substring searches catch proper names such as catering for a preschool called “Tęczowa KrAIna”.

Second, once universities established Faculties and Departments of Artificial Intelligence, every routine purchase by those units inherited the phrase in its header — alongside multi-lot tenders where only one lot involves AI. Without CPV and lot-level auditing, “Poland's AI budget” absorbs a 4.63M PLN civil renovation of faculty rooms in Częstochowa (2026/BZP 00320127 ↗), 156k PLN in general legal counsel, office chairs and conference catering, plus non-AI lots such as renewable-energy labs and burnout courses. All 12 incidental proceedings (5.22M PLN across 11 signed contracts) remain in the JSON file for auditability, excluded by default and available via a single toggle below (include & inspect all 12 incidental tenders ↓).

Third, official award notices contain human clerical errors in Section 8.2. Cross-checking Section 6.4 winning bids against Section 8.2 contract values caught two major distortions totaling 5.83M PLN: at the e-Health Centre (2026/BZP 00009631 ↗), an extra trailing zero turned a 544,000 PLN training award into 5,440,000 PLN; at Łukasiewicz–EMAG in Katowice (2024/BZP 00014936 ↗), the authority pasted the combined three-lot contract total of 468,507 PLN into Section 8.2 of every single lot, inflating the filing to 1,405,521 PLN (+937k PLN).

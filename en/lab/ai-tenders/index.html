ISSUE 01 · INVESTIGATION & DATASET Polish Public Procurement · KRS · CEIDG (BZP 2024–2026) Snapshot: 3 November 2026 · ~15 min read

60.7% of the money, zero surviving rivals: How Polish public institutions actually buy AI

Popular jokes reduce public-sector AI in Poland to two-day ChatGPT courses. Official procurement protocols tell a sharper story: nearly 90% of contracted money goes to clinical IT systems, laboratories and GPU servers — yet 60.7% of all contracted funds (19.54M of 32.20M PLN, and 77.8% in AI software — nearly double Poland's 34% national BZP single-bid average) lands with vendors facing zero surviving competitors, wiping out the 46% discount achieved whenever four or more bidders compete.

I audited all 304 linked notices in Poland's Public Procurement Bulletin from 2024–2026 (171 proceedings, 110 signed core contracts) and cross-checked every winner against corporate (KRS) and sole-trader (CEIDG) registries. Inside: all six largest pure-AI suppliers in Poland (17 of 17 contracts worth 6.29M PLN) won 100% of their money with zero surviving rivals; the #1 pure-AI supplier — whose name translates to National Medical Cloud Operator (1.96M PLN) and which was recorded five times in BZP as a “micro-enterprise” — is 100% owned by private IT giant Asseco Poland S.A.; a sole proprietorship registered 20 days before tender publication won 285.4k PLN uncontested to plug a regional hospital into the state radiology hub; and a children's hospital bought clinical decision-support AI for 306.1k PLN under the procurement code for newspapers and magazines.

Chapter 01 Sprint & mirages

Beyond the prompt-workshop myth: the 61-day KPO sprint and hotel “AI tourism”

Judging public AI procurement by raw notice titles creates an optical illusion. Civil-service courses and generative-AI seminars account for 36 of the 159 core proceedings (22.6%) (inspect all 36 training tenders ↓), yet at a median contract value of 47,000 PLN gross (~€11,000) all 26 signed training contracts across nearly three years add up to just 2.46M PLN — 7.6 per cent of core spending, alongside 0.82M PLN across 9 research and advisory contracts (2.6%, median 51.0k PLN).

Nearly nine out of ten contracted zlotys — 89.8 per cent (28.92M PLN across 75 signed contracts from 110 proceedings) — flow into hospital IT departments and university laboratories. The largest pillar is AI software and operational deployments (17.17M PLN across 45 contracts from 62 proceedings, 53.3% of value, median 255.3k PLN; filter software ↓): radiology decision support, clinical speech-to-record systems, outpatient appointment voicebots, and municipal RAG chatbots (such as Łowicz County's 680.7k PLN intranet, task-management and RAG package, 2026/BZP 00379906 ↗). The second pillar is GPU servers, diagnostic gear and lab robotics (11.74M PLN across 30 contracts from 48 proceedings, 36.5% of value, median 171.4k PLN; filter hardware ↓) — from local LLM compute nodes and automated fluorescence microscopes (620.0k PLN at MIR-PIB in Gdynia) to AI-assisted ultrasound scanners and humanoid robots.

Put against central government megaprojects, this bottom-up market reveals how stark Poland's top-versus-bottom divide really is. Every single one of the 110 domestic AI contracts signed across Poland over nearly three years (32.20M PLN gross) adds up to less than one central Ministry of Digital Affairs consortium grant for the PLLuM / HIVE language-model family (~39.49M PLN). Inside BZP itself, 97 pure-AI contracts worth 19.63M PLN (61.0% of value, 139 proceedings, median 154.2k PLN; filter pure-AI ↓) sit alongside 13 mixed turnkey contracts worth 12.57M PLN (39.0%, 20 proceedings). The largest contract in the dataset — 4.94M PLN at Jan Długosz University in Częstochowa (2026/BZP 00163288 ↗) — bundles room construction and a CT scanner for its AI Imaging Lab; the third-largest — 2.16M PLN at Łukasiewicz–WIT (2025/BZP 00543355 ↗, unplanned, 1 bid from security guard firm NOVA SYSTEM GROUP) — wraps 37 months of physical guard shifts with AI access control; and state folk ensemble “Mazowsze” bought studio monitor speakers, an audio processor and converters worth 378.7k PLN under the banner of an “integrated AI technological platform” (2025/BZP 00462052 ↗), drawing exactly 1 bid on each of its three lots priced at 100.00%, 99.96% and 99.94% of lot budgets.

EU recovery deadlines compressed that spending into a violent spring surge. Grouped by BZP notice year, signed contract value jumped 4.8×: from 4.05M PLN in 2024 (18 awards across 24 proceedings) and 8.56M PLN in 2025 (35 across 41) to 19.59M PLN through 3 November 2026 (57 across 94; or 5.6× by physical contractDate: Dec 2023 — 0.61M PLN [3], 2024 — 3.44M PLN [15], 2025 — 8.95M PLN [37], 2026 — 19.19M PLN [55]). Because Ministry of Health hospital rankings under KPO D1.1.2 arrived late in autumn 2025 ahead of summer 2026 completion cut-offs, just 61 calendar days of March and April 2026 absorbed 11.68M PLN — 36.3 per cent of all three-year AI spending in BZP.

Over 1.12M PLN in “away-day AI”: seaside SPAs, a ban on Wrocław, and a ban on professors

While trainings represent a modest share of total spending, reading their tender specifications uncovers a distinct pattern: more than 1.12M PLN in signed contracts (45.7% of all training money) funded two-day residential packages at 3- and 4-star hotels, resorts and manor houses financed largely by EU FERS and regional funds. The Regional Labour Office (WUP) in Szczecin sent 127 public employment staff to the seaside Royal Park Hotel & SPA at Wakacyjna 6 in Mielno (114.2k PLN, 2025/BZP 00191385 ↗), and paid 2,714 PLN per person (38.0k PLN for 14 managers, 2026/BZP 00206899 ↗) for “Strategic leadership in the AI era (...) incorporating work–life balance and managerial mental resilience”. Both WUP Szczecin and the Lublin Marshal's Office also inserted an explicit participation clause stating that teaching university or postgraduate courses does not count toward an AI trainer's required experience — formally disqualifying computer-science professors while admitting commercial event brokers.

Other offices were equally direct. Training its own Wrocław-based civil servants on AI tools (34.2k PLN, 2026/BZP 00243856 ↗), WUP Wrocław explicitly banned holding the workshop inside the city of Wrocław (“excluding the city of Wrocław”), forcing an out-of-town hotel stay — and in a separate two-day EURES AI course paid 106,202 PLN (53,101 PLN per day) after disqualifying two of three bids including a 63.6k PLN offer (2025/BZP 00424295 ↗). Meanwhile, the University of Siedlce booked a manor-house dinner running until 1:00 AM with bacon-wrapped shrimp, dumplings and pork aspic for an AI-themed management conference at Dwór Mościbrody (25.2k PLN, 2026/BZP 00418399 ↗), and WUP Kielce tapped KPO recovery funds to buy six pairs of in-ear AI translation earbuds at 1,483 PLN a pair (8,900 PLN after rejecting two cheaper bids, 2026/BZP 00291854 ↗).

Who contracts the most Universities and research institutes lead with 15.24M PLN (47 signed contracts across 71 proceedings, 47.3%), followed by hospitals and healthcare providers at 11.36M PLN (31 contracts across 38 proceedings, 35.3%). Together they account for 26.60M PLN (82.6%) across 78 contracts — ahead of local governments and labour offices (2.70M PLN, 21 contracts), central agencies (2.70M PLN, 8) and schools (0.21M PLN, 3).

Chapter 02 60.7% without rivals

Twenty-three bidders for slides, zero surviving rivals for 60.7% of the money

When Kielce University of Technology tendered a three-lot staff AI workshop worth 7,200 PLN in autumn 2026 (2026/BZP 00465518 ↗), up to 23 vendors piled onto a single lot (23 / 15 / 22 across lots), and an online AI course for Lower Silesian civil servants worth 29,750 PLN (2026/BZP 00428993) drew 22 bids (sort trainings by bid count ↓). Yet all 35 crowded contracts attracting 4–23 bids — nearly a third of signed awards (31.8%) — split just 3.99M PLN, or 12.4 per cent of all money (with the middle 24 contracts at 2–3 bids taking 10.18M PLN, or 31.6%).

Where real money is spent, competition collapses far beyond Poland's economy-wide BZP single-bid average of 34.0 per cent reported by the Public Procurement Office (UZP). In raw Section 6.1 counts, single-bid open tenders represent 43.3 per cent of competitive AI awards (45 of 104), absorbing 54.1 per cent of competitive value (16.71M of 30.88M PLN). Add the 6 sole-source contracts filed without Section 6.1 (1.33M PLN; inspect sole-source awards ↓), and 51 of 110 signed contracts (46.4%) worth 18.04M PLN — 56.0 per cent of the entire 32.20M PLN dataset — faced zero submitted rival bids. Inside AI software deployments, 27 of 41 open tenders (65.9%, worth 12.09M PLN; view all 27 ↓) drew a single bid; combined with 4 hospital sole-source awards (1.28M PLN), 31 of 45 software contracts (68.9%) and 13.37M of 17.17M PLN — 77.8 per cent of all AI software spending, more than double Poland's national BZP average — went uncontested on entry.

Auditing full HTML protocols across all 159 proceedings uncovers two deeper layers. First, 4 of the 45 awards coded with 1 bid in Section 6.1 (worth 2.13M PLN: Pixel Technology's 922.8k PLN and KOChM's 453.4k PLN hospital awards in Opole, upmedic's 528.9k PLN award at UMP Poznań, and StatSoft's 227.5k PLN licences at UP Poznań) were actually statutory sole-source contracts (Art. 305 PZP) where officials typed the lone invited supplier as one bid — raising true sole-source awards to 10 of 110 contracts (9.1%) worth 3.46M PLN (10.7% of spend). Those sole-source justifications stretch all the way to two same-day KPO sole-source contracts signed by the Łódź Regional Teacher Development Center in Zgierz (6,150 PLN in 2024/BZP 00620147 ↗ plus companion filing 2024/BZP 00620112 for 12,300 PLN), waiving competition because the trainer is “a specialist in the field of AI of high renown, making him a unique and irreplaceable personality”.

Second, buyers rejected 121 of 460 submitted bids (26.3% — over one in four offers), including 46 (10.0%) for abnormally low price. In 8 multi-bid tenders worth 1.50M PLN, every competing bidder was disqualified, leaving a single surviving quote. In total, 59 of 110 signed core contracts (53.6%) worth 19.54M PLN — 60.7 per cent of all public AI money — were awarded with zero surviving competitors.

The 170,000 PLN cliff, BZP's lowest-bid blind spot, and the taxpayer bill

Ranking all 104 competitive contracts by gross value splits the market cleanly in half at 170,000 PLN gross. Across the lower 52 contracts below 170,000 PLN (totaling just 3.43M PLN, 11.1% of competitive spend), the median tender draws 4.5 bids and only 21.2 per cent (11 of 52) end with one bidder. Across the upper 52 contracts at or above 170,000 PLN (27.45M PLN, 88.9% of competitive spend), the median drops vertically to 1 bid, and 65.4 per cent (34 of 52, worth 15.82M PLN) go to a lone bidder.

Regulatory irony since 1 January 2026

Raising Poland's mandatory PZP procurement floor from 130,000 to 170,000 PLN net (~209,000 PLN gross) removes from mandatory BZP visibility precisely that lower, multi-bid half of the market — leaving behind in the public bulletin the upper tier where single-bid awards are already the norm.

Matching pre-tender net cost estimates (Section 4.3) against signed gross awards (Section 8.2) puts an exact price tag on that cliff. Across homogeneous 23%-VAT software and hardware contracts (n = 31), when 1 valid bid survives (n = 18), the median gross award equals 1.230× the net estimate — 100.0% of the buyer's valuation (0% discount). With 2–3 surviving bids it dips to 93.8%, and with 4 or more surviving bids (n = 4) it plunges to 54.0% of the net estimate (0.664×, a 46.0% net discount): Kraków Academy of Fine Arts estimated its chatbot (2026/BZP 00450838 ↗) at 177.5k PLN net and paid 74.8k PLN gross (34.3% of estimate) across 19 bids, whereas USK Opole's single-bid drug-selection AI jumped from a 111.5k PLN net estimate to 203.0k PLN gross (2026/BZP 00201117 ↗), and at the 5th Military Clinical Hospital in Kraków (2026/BZP 00183217 ↗, zero qualification conditions 5.3: No), Medalion Technology P.S.A. (5,000 PLN share capital) submitted the sole bid of 232,470.00 PLN gross — matching the hospital's unpublished net valuation plus 23% VAT (189,000.00 × 1.23 = 100.000%) to the exact grosz.

Worse, BZP's summary fields systematically erase rejected cheaper bids. In 7 of the 8 multi-bid tenders where every rival was disqualified, officials entered the lone surviving winner's price as the “lowest bid” in Section 6.2 (6.2 = 6.3 = 6.4) — hiding cheaper quotes even at LCIT Lublin (2024/BZP 00443650 ↗), where two bids were explicitly rejected for abnormally low price. In the sole exception that reported Section 6.2 honestly, the Katowice Oncology Center (2025/BZP 00564260 ↗ — using tender templates with copy-pasted GDPR clauses from a surgical-suture purchase) disqualified a 238,818.60 PLN breast-cancer mammography AI bid as abnormally low and signed with Medix at 424,468.44 PLN (+185,649.84 PLN, or +77.7% higher). Częstochowa University of Technology ran a clean natural experiment: across three robotics awards won without surviving rivals by E4I (10k PLN capital; 734.9k PLN total), winning bids landed -1.23 PLN, -67.00 PLN and 0.00 PLN (90,000.00 PLN = 100.000% of budget on a 14-day window) below ceiling — whereas when the university gave 30 days and admitted 3 valid bids (2026/BZP 00446958 ↗), distributor ACTION S.A. cut the humanoid price to 54,752.48 PLN (-39.2% below budget, with possible model differences across tenders).

“Newspapers and magazines” for 306k PLN, 183k PLN kiosks, and 0% AI quality weight

Even when multiple bids arrive, custom AI quotes diverge wildly: while GPU hardware bids cluster within a 1.12× median spread, valid AI software quotes diverge by a median of 2.71× and up to 13.25× with zero rejections — at Ameryka Children's Rehabilitation Hospital's 1.28M PLN six-lot KPO package (2026/BZP 00124082 ↗, published on 23 December — Christmas Eve Eve — with zero qualification conditions 5.3: No and awarded at 180.3% of total net estimates), where three valid voicebot bids stretched from 165.0k PLN to 2.19M PLN (forcing the hospital to investigate winner Talkie for “abnormally low price” even though its quote exceeded the hospital's own 150k PLN net estimate!). Despite that valuation chaos, 63.5 per cent of competitive tenders (94 of 148) — and 72.2 per cent in AI software (39 of 54) — include zero substantive quality criteria, allowing quality to beat a lower valid price just twice in 100 awards (0 times in IT or hardware). Paying Orange Polska 916,507.44 PLN for five AI reception avatar kiosks (183.3k PLN per kiosk, 1 bid, 2025/BZP 00600276 ↗ → 2026/BZP 00137744 ↗, launched unplanned under computer display CPV codes before signing its KPO grant agreement), Warsaw's Children's Memorial Health Institute scored bids on 70% price + 20% hardware warranty + 10% spare-parts availability — assigning 0% weight to Polish speech recognition or clinical navigation accuracy while demanding prior deployment of a verbal-navigation avatar.

Elsewhere, CPV misclassifications, hyper-specific badges and 4-day schedules filtered out rivals upfront. St. Louis Children's Hospital in Kraków bought a 3-year clinical decision-support AI knowledge base for 306,065.82 PLN under KPO (2026/BZP 00212938 ↗, 8-day window, 5.3: No) classified under CPV 22200000-2 – Newspapers, journals, periodicals and magazines — invisible to software vendors monitoring IT codes and won uncontested by press subscription distributor ABE Holding (PKD 47.62.Z). Siedlce Provincial Hospital hid a cloud registration voicebot under CPV 30213300-8 – Desktop computer (2025/BZP 00497656 ↗, 7 days, 1 bid at 100.00% of estimate). The Medical University of Łódź required five Microsoft Solutions Partner designations plus a Microsoft Copilot MVP just to analyze internal employee surveys for 219.2k PLN (2026/BZP 00098633 ↗, 1 bid at 99.82% of valuation), while Gliwice Municipal Hospital (2026/BZP 00267052 ↗, 195.6k PLN, 1 bid) gave 4 calendar days (25–29 May 2026) plus an Oracle APEX developer certificate to deploy AI agents integrated with hospital SIEM and GRC security tools.

Legal HIS/PACS lock-in, paid plugs for a “free” state radiology hub, and a 20-day-old firm

Nowhere is lock-in harder than in hospitals, where 74.1 per cent of open tenders (20 of 27; filter hospitals ↓) and 77.4 per cent including sole-source awards (24 of 31 contracts worth 8.19M PLN, versus 9.5% in local governments) end without a rival. When competition watchdog UOKiK closed its multi-year hospital IT inquiry in late 2024 without cartel charges, it identified the structural trap: vendors need no illegal collusion when closed source code and exclusive interface copyrights over HIS/PACS databases create completely legal single-vendor lock-in (prompting Gorzów Provincial Hospital's CEO to file a fresh UOKiK complaint in 2025 over steep KPO integration quotes). Every hospital AI tender in our dataset naming an incumbent system (AMMS/Asseco, CliniNET, AGFA EI, Pixel, CeZ PUI) ended with zero rivals — 12 of 12 signed contracts (100.0%) plus 4 cancellations. Legnica Specialist Hospital (2025/BZP 00478008 ↗) openly required Asseco Poland S.A. authorization in an “open” tender, while the National Institute of Cardiology (2026/BZP 00213382 ↗) extended its CGM CliniNET bot deadline five times from 12 to 27 days and still got one bid (against a national median of just 9.0 days to bid versus 28.0 days for buyer review).

Nothing captures that dysfunction better than the e-Health Centre's central Intelligent Services Platform (PUI) — a ~150M PLN state radiology hub built to give hospitals free diagnostic AI models (for which CeZ signed its own second central chest X-ray AI contract worth 13.2M PLN on 27 August 2026 — 20 days after hospitals' 7 August KPO deadline had already expired). To plug local PACS/RIS archives into that “free” state platform, hospitals had to buy commercial connectors individually: out of 7 hospital PUI tenders, 0 drew two bids, 4 collapsed, and the 3 signed awards varied 3.2× in price. In Siedlce, an unplanned KPO tender (2026/BZP 00094988 ↗ → 2026/BZP 00141601 ↗, 8-day window, zero qualification requirements 5.3: No, zero deposit, 100% price) was won uncontested for 285,360 PLN by SoloSys Tomasz Buziak — a sole proprietorship registered in CEIDG on 15 January 2026, just 20 days before the tender notice. At NIO Gliwice (2026/BZP 00195811 ↗), the hospital demanded AGFA authorization, printed its exact 395,000 PLN net estimate inside Section 4.1.5, and got a lone bid from Agfa Sp. z o.o. (subcontracting the connector to CeZ's own central vendor Pentacomp) for 485,850.00 PLN gross — 100.00% of valuation to the grosz. Meanwhile, USK Opole (2026/BZP 00183188 ↗) awarded Pixel Technology 922,770 PLN sole-source bundling PUI with 12 ultrasound/X-ray hooks and a CD/DVD burner robot, pasting word-for-word the same “10-year maintenance” justification paragraph used in Opole's KOChM voicebot award. Once the H1 2026 KPO sprint ended — after hospitals crammed 26 of 38 (68.4%) of their AI tenders into six months at an 84.2 per cent (16 of 19) zero-rival rate — hospital notices fell to 1 in Q3 2026 (0 KPO), and Poland's economy-wide single-bid rate plunged from 64.7% (Q1) to 0.0 per cent (0 of 11 contracts) in Q3 2026.

Autopsy of 31 collapsed tenders: 0 PLN sole-source discounts and a +19% conflict-of-interest rebound

Section 5.2 filings across all 31 cancelled core proceedings (22.0% of 141 concluded tenders; show all 31 ↓) tell the rest of the story. Zero bids (Art. 255(1)) killed only 6 of 31 failures (19.4% — including the government's own Central IT Center, COI, getting 0 bids for Microsoft 365 Copilot licences in 2026/BZP 00082680 ↗). In 24 of 31 cancellations (77.4%), vendors submitted at least 53 bids, only for proceedings to fail over budget shock (14 of 31 = 45.2%) or 100% bid disqualification (8 of 31 = 25.8%).

Three case files stand out. Poznań University of Medical Sciences opened its clinical LLM tender between Christmas and New Year (29.12.2025 → 12.01.2026) with an opening-day budget entered as 0.00 PLN, disqualified upmedic's lone 528,900.00 PLN bid on 10 March 2026 for missing formal statements (2026/BZP 00146627 ↗, Art. 226(1)(2)(c)) — and 15 days later invoked post-cancellation sole-source Art. 305(2) PZP (2026/BZP 00172477 ↗) to award the exact same upmedic an identical 528,900.00 PLN (0.00 PLN negotiated discount). At WUP Łódź (2024/BZP 00424907 ↗), an 8-bid AI labour-market study collapsed after a research firm 10%-owned in KRS by WUP's former Deputy Director (until May 2024) and longtime tender-committee chair submitted a bid, and the office refused to spend ~9,000 PLN on external evaluators; because Round 1 bid opening had exposed every competitor's quote, in Retry #2 (2024/BZP 00534855 ↗) winner ProVision Solutions raised its own price from 63,001 PLN to 75,030 PLN (+12,029 PLN, +19.1%) and still won by 1.76 points. And UWM Olsztyn (2026/BZP 00156906 ↗, 475.2k PLN net estimate) cancelled its LLM infrastructure purchase after 3.5 months over defective specs in a notice that misspelled its own domain (umw.edu.pl) and stopped mid-sentence. Out of 26 failed initiatives, 10 (38.5%) were abandoned altogether.

Chapter 03 Leaders & KRS

All six pure-AI leaders uncontested, Asseco's “micro-enterprise”, and the 2028 bill

Strip away construction and physical guard bundles to isolate 19.63M PLN across 97 pure-AI contracts, and a striking pattern emerges at the top of the leaderboard: all six largest pure-AI suppliers in Poland (Krajowy Operator Chmury Medycznej, Stinet, Orange Polska, E4I, Vida Andrzej Tarasek and Softinet — 17 of 17 contracts totaling 6.29M PLN, or 32.0% of the entire pure-AI market) won 100% of their money with zero surviving competitors (15 uncontested on entry plus 2 after disqualifying the lone rival). The buyer side mirrors it: Poland's top three pure-AI public buyers (PARP — 1.38M PLN, NCBJ — 1.29M PLN and IPCZD Warsaw — 1.28M PLN) resolved 8 of 8 contracts (100%) with a single bid, and all 10 top pure healthcare AI software vendors faced zero rivals.

Cross-checking #1 winner Krajowy Operator Chmury Medycznej Sp. z o.o. (7 hospital contracts worth 1.96M PLN — 25.2% of all pure healthcare AI in Poland and over half of all statutory sole-source AI spend) against Poland's National Court Register (KRS 0000898941) explains why. Although its name translates to National Medical Cloud Operator, official KRS filings show the company is headquartered at Asseco Poland S.A.'s Warsaw offices on Branickiego 13, seats Asseco Vice-Presidents on its Supervisory Board, sells a product explicitly named Asseco Medical Voicebot in Opole's award notice (2026/BZP 00297319 ↗), and is 100% directly owned by private corporate giant Asseco Poland S.A. (all 26,000 shares = 1.30M PLN). Five of its seven awards (1.74M PLN) went sole-source citing exclusive copyrights to Asseco's HIS AMMS hospital software, its 12-month sole-source renewal in Poznań (2024/BZP 00574495 ↗ → 2026/BZP 00514632 ↗) jumped +16.1% year-on-year from 219.9k to 255.3k PLN, and two parallel 36-month KPO deployments signed in June 2026 diverged by +43.6% (453.4k PLN in Opole vs 651.2k PLN in Cieszyn).

Matching BZP Section 7.2 checkboxes against KRS ownership also punctures the registry's headline SME narrative. On paper, micro, small and medium-sized enterprises won 105 of 110 contracts (95.5%) and 30.21M PLN (93.8% of all spend), confirmed large corporations won just 4 contracts worth 1.82M PLN (5.6%: Orange Polska, WASKO, Siemens Healthcare and ACTION), and public universities and research institutes — which spent 15.24M PLN as buyers — won 0 of 110 contracts (0.0%) as suppliers. Yet under Annex I of EU Regulation 651/2014, wholly owned subsidiaries of large corporations are linked enterprises ineligible for SME status — whereas BZP notices recorded 100%-Asseco subsidiary KOChM five times as a “micro-enterprise” and once as “small”, and 100%-Belgian Agfa HealthCare NV subsidiary Agfa Sp. z o.o. as a “small enterprise” in Gliwice, inflating BZP's SME totals by at least 7 contracts and 2.45M PLN.

Below EU TED publication thresholds (no pure-AI award in BZP exceeds 916.5k PLN gross), multi-year operational spend also accumulates via lawful 12-month single-bid renewals: nuclear research center NCBJ paid Stinet 1.29M PLN across three annual single-bid renewals for ML network threat detection under confidential specs (Art. 280(3) PZP), while enterprise agency PARP awarded sole trader Vida Andrzej Tarasek (legacy main activity PKD 70.21.Z — public relations) two consecutive single-bid AI-EDR renewals totaling 722.0k PLN (2024/BZP 00675119 ↗ & 2025/BZP 00556574 ↗) ahead of a third open tender (2026/BZP 00513349). Consumer regulator UOKiK's 209.1k PLN VLM/LLM dark patterns detector (2025/BZP 00517087 ↗, 9-day window, 100% price) went to a 4-month-old Żyrardów company with 6,000 PLN share capital (Firstscore Sp. z o.o.) that declared in BZP subcontracting the core platform licence and integration to Estonian entity Firstscore OÜ in Tallinn. Elsewhere, NASK-PIB paid press publisher Gremi Media S.A. 356.7k PLN for licensed LLM corpora (2026/BZP 00032576 ↗), Lower Silesian NFZ spent 622.4k PLN on LLM servers, and Pharmdiver won two single-bid hospital pharmacy deployments (362.2k PLN). A maintenance bill deferred to 2028

Across the dataset, 70 of 110 signed core contracts (63.6%) worth 21.10M PLN (65.5% of spend) rely on EU co-financing — including 81.6% of healthcare proceedings (31 of 38) and 83.3% of trainings (30 of 36; filter EU-funded awards ↓). KPO grants pay for 24- or 36-month upfront subscriptions, but once durability periods expire in 2028–2029, recurring cloud inference, telephony and proprietary HIS/PACS maintenance fees land squarely on hospital operating budgets — just as conditional high-risk compliance obligations under the EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689) ↗ and the Commission's Digital Omnibus on AI ↗ take effect on 2 December 2027 (Annex III) and 2 August 2028 (Annex I medical-device software). Where uncontested renewals already rise 16.1% a year, the real fiscal test starts when Brussels' money runs out.

Chapter 04 Registry bugs & fixes

11.05M PLN in BZP noise, wrong tax IDs, and four systemic fixes

Building this database also tested the gap between asking a language model for a fluent summary and doing the forensic engineering required to stand behind every figure. Because BZP's public API silently ignores the TenderId filter and truncates batches at PageSize=500, reconstructing all 171 proceedings from 304 official notices required a deterministic Python pipeline (scripts/audit_bzp_data.py ↗) that recovered 17 missing outcome filings. Even then, summing unverified BZP keyword matches would overstate Polish AI spending by more than 11.05M PLN.

First, in veterinary institutes AI stands for Avian Influenza diagnostic kits, while substring queries catch preschool catering (“Tęczowa KrAIna”). Second, once universities created Faculties of Artificial Intelligence, routine building works inherited the phrase — producing 12 incidental proceedings worth 5.22M PLN across 11 contracts (led by a 4.63M PLN civil renovation at Częstochowa University of Technology, 2026/BZP 00320127 ↗), kept transparent behind an explicit toggle (inspect all 12 incidental tenders ↓). Third, Section 8.2 clerical errors distort totals by millions: an extra trailing zero at the e-Health Centre (2026/BZP 00009631 ↗) turned 544,000 PLN into 5,440,000 PLN (+4.90M PLN), while Łukasiewicz–EMAG (2024/BZP 00014936 ↗) pasted its three-lot total of 468,507 PLN into every single lot (+937k PLN).

BZP forms do not even validate winning contractors' tax IDs (NIP) in Section VII — exposing 8 hard identification errors across 110 signed contracts: WUP Szczecin (2026/BZP 00486087 ↗, 188.8k PLN) entered a local foundation's NIP (8513210155) onto winner MW Consulting; Ameryka Children's Hospital (2026/BZP 00124082 ↗) pasted rival OPEGIEKA's NIP onto Xcomp and signed Lot 2 under a company name (CORAZ ZDROWIEJ) replaced in KRS 9 months earlier (IDOCLY); and Łowicz County (2026/BZP 00379906 ↗) truncated the winner's NIP to 9 digits (522325929).

Four systemic fixes from 304 BZP filings

1. Mandate open clinical APIs (HL7 FHIR / DICOM) in public IT tenders. As long as public hospitals buy HIS/PACS systems with closed paid connectors and vendor-authorization gates, clinical AI add-ons will keep producing 12-of-12 zero-rival monopolies.

2. Ship free reference connectors alongside central state platforms. Building a 150M PLN central radiology hub (CeZ PUI) without open integration modules forced individual hospitals to buy separate 285k–923k PLN commercial plugs against rigid KPO deadlines.

3. Validate ezamowienia.gov.pl forms against tax and KRS registries. BZP should verify NIP checksums, enforce EU linked-enterprise rules on SME flags, alert on 10× gaps between Section 6.4 and Section 8.2, and split Section 6.2 into lowest submitted bid versus lowest non-rejected bid.

4. End 7-day holiday windows and “newspaper” CPV codes for custom AI software. Software deployments above 170k PLN should require at least 14–21 bidding days, accurate IT CPV codes, and functional evaluation benchmarks instead of 0% AI quality weight.

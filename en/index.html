03 / About

A little about me

I didn't come into IT straight from university — before that I worked physical jobs in warehouses, shops and a print shop. Only later did I work my way through tech support and testing, ERP rollouts and DevOps, all the way to software development. When the job became mostly routine, I walked away for a few years. Generative AI brought me back — because what I'm building, and why, matters more again than typing every line of code by hand.

The longer version↗
RolePlayingLife is what I call my approach to life: don't get locked into one role forever, keep trying new things — a bit like developing a character in an RPG, except without a single fixed class. RolePlayingTech is the tech side of it.

Away from the screen, I'm usually on a bike with a tent, out in the mountains or travelling with a backpack (that's where the small photos in the margins come from). This year I rode around part of the Baltic — Lithuania, Latvia, Estonia, Finland and Sweden — and the year before, from Kraków to Croatia.

04 / Work together

Things I can help with

Most days I'm building my own projects, but I also like working with other people — whether on a specific task or starting from a rough idea.

01

An idea worth testing

You have an app or an internal tool in mind and want to see if it actually holds water. I can help shape the idea, build a working first version and figure out what makes sense next.

See: Mumro, Crewly

02

Repetitive work

The same steps every week, data copied by hand between systems. I automate that with n8n or custom code so it runs reliably day to day without constant babysitting.

See: client work, Mumro

03

Content and AI

Short videos and longer formats, graphics, articles, publishing to several channels at once. I know from practice where generative AI genuinely helps and where it quickly feels plastic.

See: Świadek Dziejów

04

Software that talks to hardware

Sensors, microcontrollers, measurements, live data and controls in a browser. For when code has to work with a physical device.

See: MFC Monitoring Station

What draws me in

Unusual combinations

I'm most drawn to problems that cross a few disciplines and require learning something new first — like history and AI, or lab research, electronics and code.

How I work

Substance over noise

Generating things just because AI makes it easy gets old fast. In Świadek Dziejów, what matters is a well-told story; in tools and automation, whether it actually takes work off someone's plate.

On AI

Plainly about the tools

I use AI models and agents heavily and don't hide it. They let me build faster and step into unfamiliar territory, while architecture, testing and responsibility for the result stay with me.

02 / About

A little about me

Before I got into programming, I did all sorts of jobs, including physical work. Then came tech support, software testing, ERP implementations, systems administration, DevOps and development — eventually as a senior developer. Over time there was more routine and less curiosity, so I stepped away. A few years later, generative AI pulled me back in. These days I think more about whether the whole thing makes sense and works than about writing every line myself.

The longer version↗
RolePlayingLife is what I call my approach to life: don't get stuck in one role, keep trying new things — a bit like levelling up a character in an RPG, except there's more than one path. RolePlayingTech is the tech side of it.

Away from the screen, I'm usually on a bike with a tent or out in the mountains. This year it was around the Baltic — Lithuania, Latvia, Estonia, Finland and Sweden — and the year before, from Kraków to Croatia.

Things I can help with

I don't have one neat specialty. I'm most at home where several fields meet in a single project.

01

An idea worth testing

You have an idea for an app or a tool and want to find out whether it can become something that actually works. I can help break it down, build a first version and figure out what comes next.

See: Mumro, Crewly

02

Repetitive work

The same steps every week, data copied by hand between systems. I automate that with n8n or code, so it works day to day and you can rely on it.

See: client work, Mumro

04

Sensors, microcontrollers, measurements, live data and controls in a browser. For when code has to work with a physical device.

What draws me in

Where worlds meet

I'm most drawn to unusual problems that bring several fields together and need a bit of learning first. History and AI, environmental research and electronics, code and content.

How I work

With a purpose

When I make something, I want it to serve a purpose — even when I'm just testing a new tool. In Świadek Dziejów, AI helps, but the story being told comes first.

On AI

Openly

I work a lot with AI models and agents, and I'm open about it. They help me catch up quickly on what I don't know, while decisions, testing and responsibility for the result stay with me.

About

Some experience, a few changes of direction and a lot of curiosity. Not a CV.

01 / 05

Before

My path into technology didn't start with coding as a kid. Before IT, I worked physical jobs — in shops, warehouses, a print shop and abroad. Moving to Dublin was what prompted me to give computer science a try and study part-time. From there I worked my way through tech support, software testing, ERP rollouts, systems administration, DevOps and automation — eventually into software development and senior developer roles.

It wasn't a straight path, but it means I've seen systems from several sides — as someone who tests them, rolls them out with users, keeps them running and writes them.

Time away

What I liked most at the start was learning something new almost every day. Over time there was more routine and responsibility, and less curiosity. I stepped away from professional development for a few years, rather than carry on just because I was good at it.

Coming back

Generative AI made me want to build things again. I started by trying models and running experiments. Then came automation — with n8n and with code — paid work on tools and integrations, AI-generated video, a few clips that went viral, and more client projects. Along the way I shared tests, tutorials and thoughts online; my X account now has over 10K followers.

Eventually I got more interested in larger projects of my own than in the next one-off experiment. That's how Świadek Dziejów, Mumro and the co-created Crewly project came about. The ones I enjoy most connect distant fields: history and AI, or — with MFC — environmental engineering, electronics and software.

How I use AI

AI lets me take on work across different fields without pretending to be an expert in all of them. For me it's a way to learn, experiment and build — not a substitute for thinking. With MFC, it helped me catch up quickly on things I didn't know and build a working station for a PhD researcher at Cracow University of Technology.

With Mumro, a large part of the implementation is produced with AI models and agents. I decide what gets built, how the system should work, how to test it and what needs fixing. My software background matters more than ever here — not because I type every line, but because I can judge the whole thing and take responsibility for the result.

I automate the work I already understand and that doesn't need my attention every time. That way there's room for the next interesting problem. I also try to keep a sane pace — it's easy to overdo the number of tools and projects.

Away from the screen

RolePlayingLife is what I call my approach to life: don't get locked into one role forever, and keep trying new things. A bit like developing a character in an RPG, except there's more than one path. RolePlayingTech is the tech side of that story.

That's why, alongside building things, there are bike trips with a tent, mountain hiking and travelling with a backpack. This year I rode part of the way around the Baltic: Lithuania, Latvia, Estonia, Finland and Sweden. The year before, I went from Kraków to Croatia. Work matters, but it's only part of what I enjoy.

The small prints scattered across the site come from my own trips — click any print to take a closer look, and click again to draw another photo.

What I've worked on

  • Software development and architecture — including as a senior developer

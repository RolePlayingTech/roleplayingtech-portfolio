About

Some experience, a few changes of direction and a lot of curiosity. Not a CV.

01 / 05

Before

Before IT, I did all kinds of jobs, including physical work. I moved into tech gradually: tech support, software testing, ERP implementations, systems administration, DevOps, automation and finally development. At some point I was working as a senior developer.

It wasn't a straight path, but it means I've seen systems from several sides — as someone who tests them, rolls them out, keeps them running and writes them.

Time away

What I liked most at the start was learning something new almost every day. Over time there was more routine and responsibility, and less curiosity. I stepped away from professional development for a few years, rather than carry on just because I was good at it.

Coming back

Generative AI made me want to build things again. I started by trying models and running experiments. Then came automation — with n8n and with code — paid work on tools and integrations, AI-generated video, a few clips that went viral, and more client projects. Along the way I shared tests, tutorials and thoughts online.

Eventually I got more interested in larger projects of my own than in the next one-off experiment. That's how Świadek Dziejów and Mumro came about, and how my brother and I started Crewly.

How I use AI

AI lets me take on work across different fields without pretending to be an expert in all of them. For me it's a way to learn, experiment and build — not a substitute for thinking.

With Mumro, a large part of the implementation is produced with AI models and agents. I decide what gets built, how the system should work, how to test it and what needs fixing. My software background matters more than ever here — not because I type every line, but because I can judge the whole thing and take responsibility for the result.

I automate the work I already understand and that doesn't need my attention every time. That way there's room for the next interesting problem. I also try to keep a sane pace — it's easy to overdo the number of tools and projects.

Away from the screen

RolePlayingLife is what I call my approach to life: don't get locked into one role forever, and keep trying new things. A bit like developing a character in an RPG, except there's more than one path. RolePlayingTech is the tech side of that story.

That's why, alongside building things, there's travel, cycling, camping and sailing. Work matters, but it's only part of what I enjoy.

What I've worked on

  • Tech support and software testing
  • ERP implementations
  • Systems administration, DevOps and infrastructure
  • Software development and architecture — including as a senior developer
  • Automation and integrations — n8n and code
  • Websites and custom tools for clients
  • Generative video and AI-assisted content
  • Electronics, microcontrollers and measurements — on the MFC Monitoring Station

I also started a free Discord community for AI creators, where we try out tools, talk through ideas and share what we've learned. We open invitations from time to time and let people know on social media — worth following.
